Bologna is walked with a roof over your head. The porticoes work as a corridor between stops and, if the weather turns, the plan barely changes. This itinerary puts one walking day in order across square, towers, market and porticoes, with tortellini at the centre, and says where hours are seasonal, where sources disagree and what is closed right now.

> **In short.** Yes, one day is enough for central Bologna if you pick a route: a morning around Piazza Maggiore, San Petronio (free entry) and the Clock Tower; lunch in the Quadrilatero; early afternoon at the Archiginnasio; if you have energy left, the San Luca portico. When we opened the official booking page, the Asinelli Tower was listed as closed for maintenance: the Clock Tower is the alternative it points to. The art collections are closed on Mondays and the Archiginnasio does not open on Sundays.

## What is closed or changes in autumn? {aperto}

Three things to know before you go. First, the Asinelli Tower. Bologna Welcome's booking page carried the notice "Torre Asinelli chiusa per manutenzione nell'area", with a pointer to the Clock Tower. When it reopens, the climb needs a reservation, has 498 steps and no lift. Second, the Clock Tower has seasonal hours. From 7 September 2026 to 28 February 2027 the first entry is at 10:00 am and the last at 6:20 pm, with three entries an hour. Third, the Municipal Art Collections in the same palace have different hours (Tuesday and Thursday 2-7 pm, Wednesday and Friday 10 am-7 pm, Saturday, Sunday and holidays 10 am-6:30 pm, last entry 30 minutes before closing) and are closed on non-holiday Mondays. On top of that the tower pauses on 25 December and 10 January, with an earlier last entry at 4:20 pm on 24 and 31 December.

The practical consequence is simple: if Monday is all you have, the tower works but the collections do not, and the Asinelli needs checking. If you have a choice, pick a Tuesday to Friday. If you are left with a Sunday, drop the Archiginnasio from the plan: it is closed, and San Petronio, the basilica's museum and the Quadrilatero hold the day.

## Morning: Piazza Maggiore and San Petronio {aperto}

Start in the square where everything converges. The Basilica of San Petronio, according to its official site, has free entry and needs no reservation for individual visits. To enter the chapels of the Magi (Bolognini), San Sebastiano and San Vincenzo Ferrer you buy the paid route on the spot: 5 € full and 3 € reduced, from 10:00 am to 5:30 pm with last entry at 5:15 pm. Other single chapels and the Presbytery are visited only on request by email, at least a week ahead, at 3 € per person for each.

We re-read the hours on the official page: the basilica is open every day from 10:00 am to 6:00 pm with last entry at 5:50 pm, and the museum (free, bookshop included) from 10:00 am to 5:30 pm with last entry at 5:15 pm. The same page says that the panoramic terrace, after the restoration, is closed for good: do not look for it. The access rules matter more than the hours: shoulders and knees must be covered (no tank tops, short skirts, transparent clothes or plunging necklines), hats and caps come off, and suitcases, food, drinks and animals are not allowed, nor phone calls. The page carries a modification date at the end of June 2026: if there is a service or an event, check the notice on the day.

## Clock Tower and Palazzo d'Accursio {coperto}

It is the alternative climb to the Asinelli, and in autumn it is convenient because it opens at 10:00 am. The ticket includes the audioguide visit of the tower (about 40 minutes) and entry to the Municipal Art Collections (about an hour): 10 € full, 7 € reduced, according to Bologna Welcome. Reservation is mandatory, you show up 5 minutes before and if you are late you cannot go up on the next slot; cancellation or changes with a refund are possible up to 24 hours before. The ticket is valid for seven days, which helps because tower and collections have different hours: you can do the tower in the morning and the collections another day. Wear closed shoes (or sandals with a back strap), no flip-flops, high heels or bulky backpacks; the audioguide plays on your own smartphone with earphones, so charge it and bring them. After 7:00 pm the main door is closed and you enter by the side door under the portico. The meeting point is the Sala Farnese, on the second floor of Palazzo d'Accursio, Piazza Maggiore 6. The summit terrace is reached by a narrow stair, with a waiver signed on site and a ban under 8 years of age; there is no lift.

## Lunch in the Quadrilatero: where tortellini live {aperto}

The Quadrilatero is the centre's shop-and-market district: according to Emilia Romagna Tourism its perimeter is drawn by the crossing of via Rizzoli, via dell'Archiginnasio, via Farini and via Castiglione. On via Clavature there is one entrance of the Mercato di Mezzo, with stalls to eat from, take away or at shared tables; on via Capraie and via Drapperie you find delis and shops with mortadella, fresh pasta and sweets; on via Pescherie Vecchie, tigelle and cured meats for an aperitivo. The same source names the Osteria del Sole, in Vicolo dei Ranocchi, described as the oldest osteria in Bologna, where only wine is served and everyone brings their own food.

We do not name tortellini restaurants, because we read none on the sources we consulted. What we can give you is a yardstick: the Dotta Confraternita del Tortellino reports the deposit of 15 April 2008 at the Chamber of Commerce, which defines the classic shape, the filling, the thickness of the pasta and a final weight of 5 grams, with the pasta cut into squares 3 cm on a side. According to the same page, for about a thousand tortellini you need 10 eggs and a kilo of flour for the pasta; 300 g of pork loin, 300 g of raw ham, 300 g of mortadella, 450 g of Parmigiano Reggiano and 3 eggs for the filling; and for the broth a kilo of beef, half a hen, celery, carrot, onion and salt. Asking whether the broth is made in-house and whether the pasta is rolled daily is a more useful test than any ranking.

For ragù the source is Bologna Welcome, which gives the updated version deposited by the Accademia Italiana della Cucina at the Chamber of Commerce (the original dates from 1982): 300 g of minced beef (diaphragm, belly or shoulder), 150 g of pancetta, 50 g each of carrot, celery and onion, 300 g of tomato passata or peeled tomatoes, half a glass each of dry white wine and whole milk, broth, and about two hours of cooking.

## Early afternoon: Archiginnasio and Anatomical Theatre {coperto}

The Archiginnasio, the old university seat, is visited with an audioguide or a guided tour. According to the Musei di Bologna page, updated in July 2025 and so worth rechecking, audioguide visits run Monday to Saturday from 10:00 am to 5:40 pm, with entries every 10 minutes and a duration of 40 minutes; the full ticket is 10 € and the reduced 8 €. Guided tours cost 12 € full and leave at fixed times (for July and August, in Italian at 1:30 and 3:30 pm). Since 3 July 2025 the visits are run by Bologna Welcome and the palace is always closed on Sundays. The 8 € reduced ticket covers, among others, ages 13-18, university students with ID and holders of the Bologna Welcome Card; residents of the Metropolitan City have a 3.50 € ticket. The guided-tour times we read are only those for July and August (in English at 2:10 and 5:10 pm): we do not have the autumn ones, so if you want the guide, write or check the ticket office. Put this stop after lunch: it is entirely indoors and takes no more than an hour.

## The porticoes and the San Luca portico {coperto}

The porticoes are not a backdrop: UNESCO inscribes them as a serial site of 12 portico ensembles, and the Comune notes that the whole network is 62 km long. The listing dates from 28 July 2021. Among the twelve ensembles is "San Luca".

The San Luca portico links the city to the sanctuary. Here the sources disagree: the Comune speaks of about 3,600 metres and notes that the traditional count of 666 arches is actually slightly lower, depending on how they are counted; Bologna Welcome gives 3.5 km and 666 arches. On the route the Comune is precise: from the Arco Bonaccorsi you follow via Saragozza, flat, for about a kilometre and a half; then for more than 2 kilometres you climb, partly on steps. Bologna Welcome says to continue under the portico from Porta Saragozza. The alternative, per Bologna Welcome: bus 20 to the church of San Martino in Casalecchio di Reno, then bus 58 to the sanctuary. We did not read walking times: our estimate is no less than a couple of hours there and back on foot, and it is only an estimate.

The timetable that matters is the sanctuary's, which on the official site is this: church from 7:00 am to 6:00 pm and forecourt from 6:30 am to 6:00 pm, every day; the climb to the icon of the Madonna is closed to the public Monday to Friday from 12:30 to 2:30 pm; the bookshop is open from 10:00 am to 12:30 pm and from 2:30 to 6:00 pm. The dome with its terrace (San Luca Sky Experience) can be visited Monday to Saturday from 10:30 am to 1:30 pm and from 2:30 to 6:30 pm, on Sunday from 12:00 to 1:30 pm and from 2:30 to 6:30 pm; we did not read the price. With a 6:00 pm closing, plan to be up there with at least an hour in hand.

If you do not want to walk uphill there is the San Luca Express, the tourist train: according to Bologna Welcome it costs 15 € full and 8 € reduced, takes about an hour, includes a same-day return ticket and an on-board audioguide, and is free with the Bologna Welcome Card PLUS. The ticket is non-refundable and entitles you to the first available departure on the chosen day; the listing gives the departure stop as Piazza Galvani, while the map on the same page marks Piazza Maggiore, so check where it leaves from. For the bus: on TPER's urban lines in Bologna you can buy a ticket on board at the automatic machine when there is a green sign outside the door (coins from 10 cents to 2 euros, no change given, 90 seconds to pay), or pay with a contactless card at no extra cost; on suburban lines such as the 89 and 90 nothing is sold on board. TPER's own page tells you whether your line is urban or suburban: we did not verify it for lines 20 and 58, which Bologna Welcome names.

## How much to spend (indicative averages)

With the figures read: Clock Tower with collections 10 €, Archiginnasio with audioguide 10 €, San Petronio's paid route 5 €. In all about 25 € in tickets for one adult, with San Luca and the basilica free. These are indicative averages: they depend on discounts, days and cards such as the Bologna Welcome Card (which the sources list as free for the Asinelli and the Clock Tower).

## The most common mistakes

The first is treating the centre as open like a park: on Sunday the Archiginnasio is closed and on Monday so are the art collections, so the day is designed around the weekday, not only the places. The second is reaching San Luca late: church and forecourt close at 6:00 pm and in the middle of the day the climb to the icon stops from 12:30 to 2:30 pm on weekdays. The third is dress at San Petronio: a tank top or a hat on your head is enough to be stopped at the door under the rules. The fourth is turning up late at the Clock Tower, where whoever misses their slot does not go up on the next one and gets no refund. The fifth is looking for San Petronio's terrace, which is closed for good.

## Questions people ask before they go

**Do I need to book?** For the Clock Tower yes, it is mandatory; for the Archiginnasio the audioguide visits have entries every 10 minutes and tickets are bought online or on site at the first-floor bookshop; at San Petronio entry to the nave is free and the paid route through the chapels needs no reservation for groups under 15.

**What does it really cost?** The ticket sum is above. With the Bologna Welcome Card the sources list free entry to the Clock Tower and the Asinelli and a reduced price at the Archiginnasio: we did not read the card's price on the experience page, so compare it with single tickets before buying.

**Morning or afternoon?** Morning for the tower (first entry at 10:00 am in this season) and San Petronio; early afternoon for the Archiginnasio; San Luca only if you have at least a full hour before 6:00 pm.

## What to leave out

- Too many towers: if the Asinelli is closed, the Clock Tower is enough for a panorama.
- Mondays for museums: the art collections stay closed.
- San Luca in the rain or at day's end if you are tired: it is uphill and best in full daylight.
- The hunt for the "right" restaurant: the broth test and a Quadrilatero stall are better.

:::consiglio Book the Clock Tower for 10:00 am and do San Petronio right after: they are a few steps apart, the first runs on fixed slots and the second does not. That way you reach the Quadrilatero at lunchtime, and the Archiginnasio, with entries every 10 minutes, fits wherever it falls.
