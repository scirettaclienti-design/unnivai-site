Cagliari makes sense when you climb it. The city sits on a limestone hill above the harbour, and the old Castello district, ringed by walls and towers, is the part to take slowly. The itinerary below keeps everything in the historic centre, on foot, and says plainly what you cannot do right now: the San Benedetto market, which many visitors look for, is closed for works.

> **In short.** One day on foot between the Saint Remy bastion (free terrace), the Castello district with towers and cathedral, and the Roman amphitheatre (3 EUR, arena not visitable). The San Benedetto market has been closed for works since March 2025: vendors are in the temporary market in Piazza Nazzari.

:::tappe
- Morning | Saint Remy bastion | The Umberto I terrace, free to enter, with views over the city and the sea.
- Morning | Castello district: towers and cathedral | The San Pancrazio Tower, the Elephant Tower and Santa Maria cathedral.
- Break | Santa Croce bastion | A terrace over the Stampace district and the harbour.
- Afternoon | Roman amphitheatre | Be in by 4:30 pm in winter: the ticket office closes half an hour early.
- Evening | Via Roma and the harbour | A stroll with no fixed stops.
:::

:::consiglio Go up to Castello in the morning and keep the amphitheatre for the afternoon: in winter it closes at 5 pm, and the ticket office half an hour earlier. If you want lunch in a market, remember the historic San Benedetto market is closed: the alternative is the temporary market in Piazza Nazzari.

## Where do you start?

The simplest starting point is Piazza Costituzione, where the Saint Remy bastion stands. SardegnaTurismo says you reach it from via Roma through viale Regina Margherita. From there the bastion acts as the gateway to Castello, the upper district of the city, and at the foot of its staircases you meet the two streets locals use for strolling and shopping, via Garibaldi and via Manno.

We did not read an official timetable for the city's public transport, so we do not give lines or frequencies. You do not need them for this day: the historic centre is done on foot, with slopes and staircases (if you have reduced mobility, check the routes first).

## The Saint Remy bastion

The bastion is one of Cagliari's landmark monuments. It was built between 1896 and 1902 in a classical style on the old Sperone and Zecca bastions of Spanish times, and takes its name from the Baron of Saint Remy, the first Piedmontese viceroy. Under the main arch a staircase with two circular ramps leads to the Umberto I terrace, from which you see the whole city and the sea. SardegnaTurismo lists free admission, always.

Below the terrace is the Covered Walkway, linked to the Sperone Gallery: here you pay. The city's tourism site lists a full ticket from 3 EUR, reduced from 2 EUR and free for children under 6. On hours there is a limit we want to flag: the official page gives 10 am-1 pm and 3-7 pm, but with a validity written as ending on 27 April 2025, so it refers to a past season, while a 2022 city notice gives Tuesday to Sunday 10 am-6 pm. We cannot tell you for sure which schedule applies today: check before you go up.

## Castello: towers, walls and cathedral

The Castello district sits at the top of the city, between largo Carlo Felice, piazza Yenne, via Manno and via Garibaldi. The walls run around the perimeter of the old district and include the Elephant Tower and the San Pancrazio Tower. According to SardegnaTurismo, the fortifications can be visited freely.

**San Pancrazio Tower.** It was built in 1305 by the architect Giovanni Capula, in white limestone quarried from the Bonaria hill. At its base opens the San Pancrazio gate, one of the entrances to Castello.

**Elephant Tower.** It was completed in 1306-1307. The name comes from the statue of the elephant, the city's symbol, set on a corbel on the outer side, facing the harbour. The city's tourism site describes it as a four-level structure more than thirty metres high, an excellent lookout against attacks from the sea. Walking through under the portcullis is today one of the always-open routes into Castello. Climbing the tower needs a ticket: we did not read an updated winter schedule (a 2022 notice gives Tuesday to Sunday 10 am-5 pm), so check first.

**Santa Maria cathedral.** It is the district's most famous monument. It was built in the 13th century and altered several times; it is first mentioned in 1255. SardegnaTurismo describes it as 35 metres long, 34 wide and 32 high, with a neo-Romanesque marble façade of 1931 (the baroque one of 1704 was dismantled). Inside is Guglielmo's pulpit, carved for Pisa cathedral and moved to Cagliari in 1312. Under the altar is the Shrine of the Martyrs, a crypt cut into the rock in 1618 that holds 192 relics. We did not verify hours or admission.

:::foto cagliari-cattedrale | The Castello district seen from a distance, with the dome of Santa Maria cathedral

**Santa Croce bastion.** After the cathedral it is worth stopping here. SardegnaTurismo describes it as an elegant terrace and a nightlife spot, overlooking the Stampace district and the sea of the harbour. The church of Santa Croce, which gives it its name, was a synagogue converted into a Catholic church in 1492.

## The Roman amphitheatre

The amphitheatre is the most important public building of the Roman period in Cagliari, according to the city's tourism site. It stands on viale Sant'Ignazio da Laconi. One piece of information changes the experience: until restoration is finished the whole building cannot be visited and the arena and underground areas are closed. What you see, therefore, is mostly the seating cut into the rock.

The hours given by the city are Monday to Sunday 10 am-5 pm from 1 October to 27 April (ticket office half an hour before closing), and 10 am-1 pm and 3-7 pm from 28 April to 30 September. A 2022 city notice talked instead about opening on Wednesday, Friday and Sunday: it is an older document, and we cite it only because sources do not match. Prices: 3 EUR full, 2 EUR reduced, 1 EUR schools (indicative averages). If you want to see more than one site, the combined 8 EUR ticket, valid one week, covers the amphitheatre, Crypt of Santa Restituta, Viper's Cave, Elephant Tower, Covered Walkway and Sperone Gallery.

## What about the San Benedetto market?

It is the question many visitors ask, because for locals San Benedetto is "the Market". Today, however, you will not find it at its historic site. The city's website says the market, opened on 1 June 1957 (two levels, about 4,000 square metres), has been closed for renovation since March 2025 and that vendors moved to the temporary market in Piazza Nazzari. According to Cagliari News (23 October 2026), reopening of the new building is now set for no earlier than 2028, with an overall budget of more than 31 million euros plus more than 8 million for the temporary market. The city listed the historic market's address as via Francesco Cocco Ortu 46. We did not read the temporary market's hours: if you want to go, check first.

## How does the day fit together?

An order that uses only the hours we read: morning at the Saint Remy bastion and then on foot to Castello, with the towers and the cathedral; lunch break, for example at the temporary market in Piazza Nazzari; afternoon at the amphitheatre, going in by 4:30 pm if you visit between October and April. In the evening the Santa Croce bastion terrace is the right place to see the city from above. The times in this outline are our suggestion, not official hours.

## What if it rains?

A Cagliari itinerary that is almost all outdoors suffers in the rain. The indoor places we read about are the cathedral, the Covered Walkway (under the bastion terrace) and the Elephant Tower, but hours must be checked first. The amphitheatre, cut into the rock, is visited in the open: if it rains, postpone it.

## What to skip

Three choices to lighten the day. First: do not look for the San Benedetto market at its historic site. Second: do not plan on entering the amphitheatre arena, because it is not visitable. Third: do not automatically buy the 8 EUR combined ticket. The amphitheatre and the Covered Walkway cost from 3 EUR each: the combined ticket only makes sense if you enter several of the six included sites, which is hard in a single day.
