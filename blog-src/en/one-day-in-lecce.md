Lecce is a city to walk with your chin up. Lecce baroque does not come across in a photo: it needs a slow pace, pale stone that changes colour with the light, and a sweet stop or two. This itinerary puts one walking day in order, with few stops and the reason for each, and says plainly where hours change with the season.

> **In short.** Breakfast with a pasticciotto, the amphitheatre in Piazza Sant'Oronzo and the Faggiano museum in the morning, the castle around midday, the baroque route (Duomo, Santa Croce) in the afternoon. From October to March the churches close at 6 pm and the amphitheatre at about 3:30 pm, so the order of the stops matters. Avoid Mondays.

## Before you go: days and hours to check {aperto}

You can see Lecce in a day only if the opening hours line up, and in autumn they do not line up on their own. The churches on the baroque route are open every day from 9 am to 9 pm according to the official site, but between October and March they close at 6 pm, with entry allowed until 15 minutes before. The amphitheatre, on the Ministry of Culture site, is listed as closed on Monday and open 9 am to 3:30 pm on winter hours; the online ticket office, when consulted, showed 9 am to 3 pm for ticket sales and 9 am to 3:30 pm for the public. The castle is open Tuesday to Sunday.

The practical consequence is simple: whatever closes early (the amphitheatre, the castle tour) goes in the morning, whatever stays open until evening (the churches, the Faggiano museum) goes in the afternoon. If Monday is the only day you have, drop the amphitheatre and the castle and keep the baroque route and the Faggiano.

## Morning: breakfast with a pasticciotto {aperto}

Start where locals start. A pasticciotto is a shell of shortcrust pastry filled with custard and baked; it is recognised as a traditional agri-food product of Puglia and, according to the Italian Wikipedia entry, people in the Salento habitually have it for breakfast in local cafés and pastry shops. People argue about whether it should be eaten hot: some want it straight from the oven, but the same source points out that a good handmade pasticciotto can be enjoyed at room temperature too.

One story not to take as gospel: the tale that it was born by accident in 1745 in Galatina at the Ascalone bakery is a legend told in several versions, and the article that launched it, from 2005, cannot be verified. The oldest document known for the name of the shape, a 1707 inventory in Nardò, only mentions copper moulds. If someone tells you the date as a fact, smile and order.

We do not name places: in a city with this many pastry shops, walk into one where the counter is full and people are eating standing up.

## The Roman amphitheatre in Piazza Sant'Oronzo {aperto}

It is the first surprise of the day: at the most central point of the city, below the level of the square, there is a Roman amphitheatre. According to the Ministry of Culture it measured about 102 by 82 metres and could hold between 12,000 and 14,000 people; it was brought to light from 1900 by the archaeologist Cosimo De Giorgi, and today about a third of it can be seen, while the rest lies under the square and the surrounding buildings.

Here the sources do not agree, and it is worth knowing. The Ministry site and the official ticket office list a full ticket at 5 € (reduced 2 €), while the tourism portal Visit Lecce says 10 € for adults. We suggest trusting the official ticket office and checking at the moment of purchase. The ticket office also states that entry is free on 4 November 2026 and on the first Sunday of each month, and that the Roman Theatre is temporarily closed for refitting works. The Roman Theatre Museum, on the Comune's page, is listed as open Monday to Saturday from 9:30 am to 1 pm, with afternoon visits by reservation only. Dates and works change: check first.

## Faggiano museum: the indoor breather {coperto}

The Faggiano museum is open every day from 9:30 am to 8 pm and costs 5 € for single visitors, with discounts for young people (free from 0 to 8 years, 3 € from 8 to 16, according to the site). Of the museums on this route it has the most convenient schedule: it stays open until 8 pm, so it works as a wildcard. We put it in mid-morning because it is small and does not force any awkward scheduling; if the weather turns, you can move it a few hours either way and lose nothing.

## Charles V Castle: the one stop with a fixed time {coperto}

The castle can only be visited with a guide, in turns of about 50 minutes. From October to May, Tuesday to Sunday, tours leave at 10:30, 11:30 and 12:30 and then at 4, 5, 6 and 7 pm. The guided tour costs 10 € full and 6 € reduced and can be bought online on the site, which also lists exhibitions with a separate ticket (see below). We suggest the 12:30 tour: it does not clash with anything else and leaves lunch right after. If you turn up without booking, go to the ticket office a few minutes before departure, as the site advises.

## Afternoon: Piazza del Duomo, Santa Croce and the single ticket {coperto}

The heart of Lecce baroque is done with one ticket: the Duomo with its crypt, the Basilica of Santa Croce, the churches of San Matteo and Santa Chiara, the old Seminary with its cloister and the Museum of Sacred Art. The official site lists 11 € full, 5 € reduced and 24 € family; if you also want the view from the Duomo bell tower, the full ticket costs 21 € (13 € reduced, 48 € family), with climbs every 20 minutes. The ticket office is at Piazza Duomo 5.

Give the façade of Santa Croce some time before going in: it is where the stone says the most. And do not make the mistake of leaving the churches for last at 5:50 pm: with closing at 6 and entry allowed until 15 minutes before, you risk paying for a ticket and seeing little.

## Lunch: what to order in Lecce {aperto}

The Italia.it guide, the official tourism portal, lists orecchiette with tomato sauce, ciceri e tria (diamond-cut pasta, part boiled and part fried, with chickpeas), sagne 'ncannulate (twisted ribbons with tomato, ricotta and basil) and the rustico leccese, two discs of puff pastry filled with béchamel, tomato and mozzarella, among the typical dishes. For fish it points to scapece, fried oily fish dressed with vinegar and saffron. If you do not want a sit-down lunch, the rustico is the easiest format to fit between the castle and the churches. For something sweet, besides the pasticciotto, the same guide mentions the fruttone: shortcrust with jam and almond paste, covered in dark chocolate. And caffè alla leccese is an espresso served with ice and almond milk.

## Do you need to book the castle? Is the full ticket worth it?

The castle website says visits are guided only and that you can buy online or go to the ticket office a few minutes before departure; it does not say how many places each tour has, so we cannot tell you whether tours fill up in October. If you want the 12:30 tour to fit lunch around it, buying online is the prudent choice.

On the baroque-route full ticket the difference is clear: 21 € instead of 11 €, so 10 € more for the Duomo bell tower climb, with departures every 20 minutes, and the full ticket's hours start at 10 am instead of 9. It makes sense on a clear day; under low cloud or rain the extra 10 € buys little. We did not find the time of the last climb: ask at the ticket office in Piazza Duomo.

## What exhibitions are on at the castle now?

On the castle site, besides the guided tour, two exhibitions are listed with a separate ticket. "Bella Figura" was scheduled from 5 June to 23 August 2026, so it is over. "La materia dei sogni" (The Stuff of Dreams) is listed from 17 July 2026 to 10 January 2027, last entry at 7 pm: 8 € full and 4 € for ages 18-25. There is also a combined castle-plus-exhibitions ticket at 15 € full, sold at the ticket office only: it costs less than the guided tour (10 €) plus the exhibition (8 €), which makes 18 €. If the exhibition interests you, the combined ticket saves 3 €.

## Common mistakes in Lecce

- Reaching the amphitheatre after 3 pm: on the official ticket office page ticket sales stop at 3 pm and visitors leave by 3:30 pm.
- Counting on Monday: the amphitheatre and the castle are closed, and according to the Ministry page the Castromediano museum does not open either.
- Looking for the Roman Theatre: the ticket office warns that it is temporarily closed for refitting works.
- Leaving the churches for 5:50 pm: with closing at 6 pm from October to March and entry allowed until 15 minutes before, you may not get in.
- Trusting a single price: for the amphitheatre, as said above, the sources do not agree.

## If you have spare time: the Castromediano museum and the walls

The Castromediano museum, on Viale Gallipoli, is presented by Italia.it as the oldest archaeological museum in Puglia, with a small picture gallery and a lapidary, and finds from the Messapian and Roman periods. The Ministry of Culture page lists it as open every day except Monday, from 9 am to 7 pm between October and May, with last entry half an hour before closing. Mind that this page shows a 2022 update and gives no price, so confirm hours and ticket before going. Also according to Italia.it, the city walls were commissioned by Charles V against Turkish raids, and Porta Napoli, a gate shaped like a triumphal arch, is one of the entrances to the historic centre. Among the churches, the guide points to San Matteo (included in the single ticket) for its unusual façade: a concave, smooth upper part and a convex lower part decorated with scales and rustication.

## How much to spend (indicative averages)

Adding up the figures read on the official sites: amphitheatre 5 €, Faggiano museum 5 €, baroque route 11 €, castle 10 €. That is about 31 € in tickets for one adult, about 41 € if you add the Duomo bell tower; with the combined castle-plus-exhibition ticket (15 € instead of 10 €) it comes to about 36 €. These are indicative averages and depend on reductions, free days and temporary exhibitions: they are not a promise.

## Plan B: if the weather changes

In the rain the plan rewrites itself naturally. The amphitheatre and the square take a quarter of an hour even under clouds; the rest is almost all indoors. Put the Faggiano and the castle in the worst hours and leave the churches for a clear spell. In the widget above you can try the swap.

## What to leave out

- Too many museums: with the amphitheatre, the Faggiano and the castle you already have three indoor visits. One fewer is fine if you want to walk.
- Lunch on the main square: two blocks from the most photographed squares are enough to change quality and prices.
- The 5:50 pm rush: the churches deserve calm.

:::consiglio If Monday is all you have, flip the idea: keep the Faggiano museum and the baroque route, leave the amphitheatre and the castle for another day. And if you can choose, entry to the amphitheatre is listed as free on Sunday 1 November 2026 (first Sunday of the month) and Wednesday 4 November 2026, according to the official ticket office: on those days be in the queue when it opens at 9.
