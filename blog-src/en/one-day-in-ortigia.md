Ortigia is small, and that is exactly why it is easy to get wrong: you cross it in a rush trying to see everything and leave feeling you have not really looked at it. This itinerary does the opposite. Few stops, an order that makes sense and room to pause.

> **In short.** Market in the morning, Duomo and square around midday, seafront and the Fountain of Arethusa in the late afternoon. The rest you choose by mood.

:::tappe
- Morning | Ortigia market | The starting point: arrive early, taste standing up and leave before the crowd.
- Late morning | Duomo and square | The Greek temple inside the Baroque cathedral, and five minutes on the square.
- Lunch | Side streets | Two blocks from the square are enough to change quality and prices.
- Early afternoon | Inner streets and the Giudecca | Without a plan, keeping an eye out for guided visits.
- Late afternoon | Fountain of Arethusa and seafront | As far as Castello Maniace, in the low light.
:::

:::widget time

## Morning: the market

Ortigia's market is a good starting point: it runs in the morning and it is where you immediately grasp the island's atmosphere. Arrive early, taste something standing up and leave the stalls before they get crowded.

## Late morning: Duomo and square

Syracuse Cathedral is built around the remains of a Greek temple dedicated to Athena: the Doric columns are still visible in the side walls. It is a rare example of centuries layered in the same building. The square in front, with its façade, is worth five minutes before moving on.

## Lunch without hurry

Ortigia lends itself to long lunches. Avoid trattorie with a tourist menu at the door and look for the side streets: two blocks from the square are enough to change quality and prices.

## Early afternoon: the inner streets

Let yourself get lost in the maze of lanes. In the Giudecca area you find traces of the Jewish presence in Syracuse. If guided visits interest you, check days and times in advance.

## Late afternoon: Fountain of Arethusa and seafront

The Fountain of Arethusa is a freshwater spring a few steps from the sea, tied to an ancient myth. From here the walk along the sea leads to Castello Maniace, at the tip of the island. The best moment is when the light drops.

## What to leave out

- Too many museums in the same day: one, well chosen, is enough.
- Out-and-back trips: follow a loop.
- The idea of "having seen everything": Ortigia gets better with every return.
