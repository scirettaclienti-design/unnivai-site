Perugia sits on a hilltop and Assisi on the side of a hill, so a car looks handy until you start looking for parking. In fact the whole trip works with a regional train, a bus and a small automated metro, the Minimetrò. This itinerary lays out two days without a car, with the transfers explained and the hours that change the plan in November.

> **In brief.** Day one in Perugia: Minimetrò from the station to the old town, Piazza IV Novembre and the National Gallery of Umbria (closed on Mondays). Day two in Assisi: regional train of about 20 minutes, then the Assisi Link bus (1.30 € each way) to the centre, the Basilica of St Francis and Piazza del Comune. Check the hours: the Basilica has partial closures in mid-November.

## Day 1: Perugia, from the train to the centre by Minimetrò

Getting to Perugia without a car is the easy part. The railway station is linked to the Minimetrò's Fontivegge stop: the official line diagram marks a train icon there, and the National Gallery's page confirms it, pointing to the Minimetrò from Fontivegge station to the Pincetto terminal, already in the historic centre. The line is 3,020 metres long, climbs about 160 metres with a maximum gradient of 11.5%, and runs on average every 2.5 minutes. Let it do the climbing.

Tickets are simple: the integrated Unico Perugia fare is valid on the Minimetrò (Pian di Massiano to Pincetto), on city buses and on regional trains within the municipality of Perugia, with a single ride, a 24-hour tourist ticket and 10-ride multi tickets. Always validate your ticket. We did not read the price on an official page, so we do not write one: check at the station ticket office or the Minimetrò counters.

Hours are the first place where sources disagree. On the Minimetrò's official site the last ride is at 21:05 on weekdays and 20:45 on Sundays and holidays, with service starting at 7:00 (9:00 on Sundays); the Visit Perugia page, updated in 2024, says 7:00-21:20 Monday to Saturday and 8:30-20:30 on Sunday. We trust the operator's own site, which is more recent. The same site says that every Saturday until 26 December 2026 service runs until 1:05.

A note for anyone coming right now: Eurochocolate 2026 runs from 13 to 22 November and the Minimetrò has special hours. On Saturdays 14 and 21 November it runs from 7:00 to 1:05; on Sundays 15 and 22 from 7:30 to 20:45; on the festival weekends there are extra buses on the same ticket, and from Friday to Sunday bicycles are not allowed on the cars. It is a good reason to visit Perugia on a weekday, when the line keeps its normal hours.

## Morning: Pincetto, Corso Vannucci and Piazza IV Novembre

Get off at Pincetto and go up into the centre. The official diagram shows escalators and an inclined lift at the terminal; the Visit Perugia page adds that from the Cupa stop the Pellini escalators lead towards the Torre degli Sciri and Porta Santa Susanna, and from there the walk to Corso Vannucci is, in their words, uphill but beautiful. In practice you need nothing beyond the Minimetrò, and you can choose between the easy way and the slow way.

The destination is Piazza IV Novembre, with the Fontana Maggiore and the Palazzo dei Priori. The Palazzo is your afternoon stop: it houses the National Gallery of Umbria and, according to the Ministry of Culture, is the only Italian public palace that hosts a major national museum, and it has been the seat of the city government since medieval times. Before going in, stop in the square: the Gallery is right above you, and from here the rest of the centre is on foot.

## Afternoon: National Gallery of Umbria

The Gallery is on the upper floors of the Palazzo dei Priori, Corso Vannucci 19. From 9 November 2026 to 1 March 2027 it is closed on Monday and open Tuesday to Sunday from 8:30 to 19:30, with the ticket office closing at 18:30: if you arrive after 18:30 you do not get in, even with hours left before closing. Three special Monday openings are planned: 7 December 2026, 28 December 2026 and 4 January 2027.

The full ticket is 10 €, rising to 12 € during exhibitions. The reduced ticket is 2 € for EU citizens aged 18 to 25, 6 € for Touring Club Italiano members, and free under 18. On the first Sunday of the month entry is free if you go straight to the ticket office (the 2 € pre-booking is optional for individuals): the next one is Sunday 6 December 2026. The audio guide with a permanent-collection ticket is 15 € in total, and the cloakroom is free.

What is inside, according to the Ministry: 40 rooms over about 3,500 square metres, eight centuries of art, more than 3,000 works in the collection of which more than 500 are on display. The signature masterpiece, in room 13, is Piero della Francesca's Sant'Antonio polyptych; the museum also holds the richest and most complete collection of Perugino in the world and a room dedicated to Pinturicchio. It is not a museum to rush: choose two or three rooms and stay there.

For the evening we do not name places: we have not verified any address, and Corso Vannucci and the streets around it are enough for a stroll before dinner.

## Day 2: Assisi, train plus bus

The regional train from Perugia to Assisi covers about 19 km. The booking site Omio, a third-party source and not the operator, shows between 17 and 25 minutes, with prices starting from about 4 € each way. The number of daily trains differs from page to page on the same site, so we do not print a frequency: open the timetable for your day on Trenitalia and pick one.

Assisi railway station is not in the centre, which is why the Assisi Link exists. According to Trenitalia, the bus leaves from the forecourt in front of the station and takes you to the historic centre; you can buy it with the train by choosing Assisi Centro as your destination, at 1.30 € for a single trip (added to the rail fare), with no discounts on the bus part. The bus-only ticket is not sold on Trenitalia channels, but Omio says you can buy the standard urban ticket on board with a contactless card for 1.30 €: validate it every time you board.

## Morning: Basilica of St Francis

Go to the Basilica first because it is the stop with the most rules. The official site lists the lower church from 6:00 to 18:30 (closing at 17:30 on Wednesdays), the upper church from 8:30 to 18:00 and the crypt with the Saint's tomb from 6:00 to 18:15. The Ministry of Culture page gives 8:30-18:45 every day, but it was last updated in 2022: of the two, the Basilica's website wins. That site also carries two November notices: on 14 November the lower church cannot be visited from about 10:30 to 12:30, and on 17 November the upper church opens at about 13:00.

The Basilica is made of two churches built one on the other: construction began in 1228 and the church was consecrated in 1253, according to the Ministry. In the upper church, on the lowest band of the nave, you find the twenty-eight Stories of St Francis attributed to Giotto and other masters; in the lower church, among other works, the chapels frescoed by Simone Martini and Pietro Lorenzetti.

## Afternoon: Piazza del Comune and the Temple of Minerva

From the Basilica walk to Piazza del Comune. The Temple of Minerva, according to the Visit Assisi tourist portal, was built in the second half of the 1st century BC, was turned into a church in the Middle Ages, and still has its six fluted Corinthian columns. It is open in winter time from 8:00 to 20:00, with free entry, and is not accessible to visitors with reduced mobility. Goethe, on his 1786 journey, called it the first intact ancient monument he had seen.

:::foto assisi-piazza-del-comune | Piazza del Comune in Assisi, with the columns of the Temple of Minerva on the left

If you have time left, Trenitalia's Assisi Link page also mentions the Basilica of Santa Chiara, with the saint's relics, and the Eremo delle Carceri set in nature: two stops we chose to leave out because we did not read up-to-date official hours. If you add them, do them early in the morning or the next day.

## How much to spend (indicative averages)

Putting together the figures we read: National Gallery 10 € (12 € with exhibitions), train from about 4 € each way according to Omio, Assisi Link bus 1.30 € each way according to Trenitalia. For an adult that is about 10 € for the museum plus 10-11 € for train and bus, return, not counting the Minimetrò ticket, which we did not find on an official page. The churches and the Temple of Minerva are free to enter. These are indicative averages, not a promise.

## Common mistakes in Perugia and Assisi

- Planning Perugia on a Monday: the National Gallery is closed, apart from the three special openings in December and January.
- Arriving at the Gallery after 18:30: the ticket office closes an hour before the museum.
- Taking the train to Assisi and then looking for the centre on foot from the station: you need the Assisi Link bus, which leaves from the forecourt in front of the station.
- Counting on the Basilica on 14 and 17 November without checking the notices: there are partial closures.
- Trusting a single timetable: for the Minimetrò and the Basilica the sources disagree, as said above.

## Plan B: if your day changes

If it rains the plan holds: the Minimetrò, the Gallery and the Basilica are mostly indoors, and the Temple of Minerva takes a few minutes. If you are tired, cut Piazza del Comune and stop at the Basilica. If Eurochocolate makes Perugia too crowded, move the Perugia day to a weekday and keep Assisi for the weekend.

## What to leave out

- Too many stops in one day: two cities in a day only works if you give up one of them.
- The car: in Assisi, according to Visit Assisi, only authorised vehicles enter the historic centre; in Perugia you can leave it at Pian di Massiano (free parking, according to the Minimetrò), but you do not need it for this plan.
- Places we do not know: we name none, because we have not verified them.
