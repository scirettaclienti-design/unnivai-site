You can see Rome in two days if you accept one rule: do not try to see everything. The city is made of layers, and the more you try to cover it, the more time you spend between stops. The secret is to group it by area.

> **In short.** Day one: ancient Rome. Day two: historic centre and Vatican, in an order that depends on bookings. Always check opening hours and access rules.

:::tappe
- Day 1 · morning | Colosseum, Forum and Palatine | A single route: book ahead to avoid the queue.
- Day 1 · lunch | Monti | A more genuine neighbourhood a few minutes from the Colosseum.
- Day 1 · afternoon | Capitoline Hill and the centre | The view over the Forum, then on foot into the historic centre.
- Day 2 · morning | Vatican Museums and St Peter's | To be booked, and the longest part of the day.
- Day 2 · afternoon | Pantheon, Navona, Trevi | All close, a few minutes' walk apart.
- Day 2 · evening | Trastevere | A stroll with no plan.
:::

:::consiglio Put the booked stops on the same day and keep the other one free: if it rains or you are tired, you move the free part, not the one with a ticket.

:::widget days

## Day 1: ancient Rome

**Morning: Colosseum, Forum and Palatine.** They are close and are often visited on a single route: check the official site for how tickets and hours work. Booking ahead avoids the worst of the queue.

**Lunch around Monti.** A few minutes' walk from the Colosseum, it is a neighbourhood with less touristy places than the area immediately around the monuments.

**Afternoon: Capitoline Hill and the centre.** Climb the Capitoline Hill for the view over the Forum and continue on foot towards the historic centre.

## Day 2: historic centre and Vatican

One decision matters here: the Vatican takes time and usually a booking. If you want to see it, put it in the morning and keep the rest of the day lighter.

**Morning: Vatican Museums and St Peter's.** Check closing days and entry rules on the official site.

**Afternoon: Pantheon, Piazza Navona and Trevi.** They are all close, a few minutes' walk from one another. For the Pantheon, check the entry rules.

**Evening: Trastevere.** A stroll with no plan is the right way to finish.

## The three rules that make the difference

1. **Group by area.** No zig-zagging.
2. **Book the most requested places,** leave the rest free.
3. **Leave one empty hour each day.** Rome gets better when you are not in a hurry.
