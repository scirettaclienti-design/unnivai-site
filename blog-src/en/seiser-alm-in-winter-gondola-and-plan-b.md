Seiser Alm, called Alpe di Siusi in Italian, is easy to reach in three ways: on foot from the valley, by car with many restrictions, or by the gondola that leaves from Seis am Schlern (Siusi allo Sciliar) and takes about 15 minutes to reach the plateau. In winter the gondola is the sensible choice, but it has a fixed season and in late November it has not opened yet. This guide is for anyone who wants the plateau without skis: when you can go up, what it costs, what you can walk, and what to do if the weather changes.

> **In breve.** Yes, you can visit Seiser Alm in winter without skiing. In 2026/27 the gondola runs from 4 December 2026 to 4 April 2027, daily 8 am to 6 pm, and a return ticket is 31 € for an adult. Without skis you walk the 60 km of prepared winter trails, go sledding or take a horse-drawn sleigh. Plan B is the valley (Seis, Kastelruth, Völs, Tiers) or another day; do not count on mountain huts in late November.

## When can you really go up (and what happens before)

The most useful point for anyone planning today: the Seiser Alm gondola is closed until 3 December 2026. The Seiser Alm website gives the winter 2026/2027 season as 4 December 2026 to 4 April 2027, with rides every day from 8:00 am to 6:00 pm. Dolomiti.it, in its article on the opening of the ski season published on 6 October, gives the same dates for the Val Gardena/Seiser Alm area. Some other Dolomiti Superski areas, such as Cortina, Kronplatz, Val di Fiemme and San Martino di Castrozza, open earlier, on 28 November.

One caution about the sources: the "winter holiday" page of the same website still shows the previous season's dates (5 December 2025 to 7 April 2026). It looks like text that has not been updated, but we cannot be sure: we trust the gondola page itself, confirmed by Dolomiti.it, and we suggest you recheck that page before going up. The same applies to the timetable PDF, which on the site is still the winter 2025/2026 one.

## How much is the gondola (indicative averages, official fares)

These are the 2026/2027 prices read on the official page. For an adult, a single ride costs 22 € and a return 31 €. For families, defined as two adults with children born after 1 January 2009, a single is 47 € and a return 66 €. Juniors (born 2009 to 2018) pay 14 € and 19 €, seniors (born 1961 or earlier) 19 € and 27 €, and children born from 2019 ride free. There is also a multi-ride card at 73 € (three return rides per season, non-transferable).

The website warns that discounts are only available at the gondola ticket offices, with the person present and valid ID; otherwise you pay the full fare. Dogs and bikes carry a surcharge (dog 5 € single and 10 € return, bike 6 € and 12 €), and dogs must be on a lead and muzzled. When we read the page, it said online ticket sales were not available because of a technical problem on the website: plan to buy at the ticket office.

The ride is already included in four products: the Seiser Alm/Val Gardena ski pass, the Dolomiti Superski pass, the Nordic Pass and the Combi Card. For non-skiers, the site suggests the Combi Card (sledding, winter hiking, snowshoeing): the 3-in-7 costs 73 € and gives three return rides within seven days plus shuttle buses and the Almbus; the 7-day Combi Card costs 95 € for seven consecutive days. One day does not justify it: for that, the 31 € return ticket is enough.

## What about driving? The rules change between seasons

Seiser Alm covers 56 km², is a protected landscape and is part of the Sciliar-Catinaccio Nature Park, so private access is limited. In winter, when the gondola is running, the road is closed to private traffic from 9:00 am to 5:00 pm: you can drive up to Compatsch before 9 or after 5, and drive down at any time. For the parking areas on the plateau, P1 Spitzbühl and P2 Compatsch, online booking is compulsory and both are paid. From 4 December 2026 to 4 April 2027 the site gives 15 € a day for a car at P1 and 31 € a day at P2. If both are already full before 9 am, the San Valentino checkpoint closes and you cannot pass.

In the shoulder period from 3 November to 3 December, with the gondola closed, the road to Compatsch stays open all day, but parking must still be booked and prices drop: 1 € a day at P1 and 15 € at P2 for a car. As an alternative to the car there is bus line 179, with three runs in the morning and three in the afternoon, free with the South Tyrol Guest Pass. One point on the page is ambiguous: in one passage it says booking opens "from three days before" and in another "up to six days before". We do not pick one: look at the booking form directly. In any case the official advice is the gondola: a free outdoor car park at the valley station (no parking from 10 pm to 6:30 am) and two paid garages at 2.50 € an hour and 10 € a day.

## What to do on the plateau without skis

The official site describes 60 km of prepared winter trails, with views of the Langkofel, Plattkofel, Schlern and Rosengarten, and calls them avalanche-protected. You need sturdy boots and warm clothes; snowshoes are not necessary, though you can use them. The rules that matter: stay on the marked trails, do not walk on cross-country or ski tracks, and take your litter with you. After heavy snowfall or changing temperatures, the site admits that not all trails may be prepared right away. For emergencies it gives 112.

:::foto siusi-sassopiatto-inverno | The Plattkofel (Sassopiatto) in winter, seen from the west

Among the trails is a themed one dedicated to Paula Wiesinger and Hans Steger, pioneers of skiing and mountaineering. It crosses the plateau from the upper gondola station to Saltria, at the foot of the Langkofel and Plattkofel, with panels on geology, flora and fauna, human settlement and legends. We did not find its length or walking time, so we do not write them.

For something else, the site lists horse-drawn sleigh rides, sledding (we did not verify where you rent a sled or the price), ice skating and, in the wider area, 181 km of ski slopes, 80 km of cross-country tracks between 1,800 and 2,200 metres, and a 50 km network of snowshoe routes around Tiers. The site describes the scenery as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Dolomites; UNESCO describes the Dolomites as a serial property of nine areas with 18 peaks above 3,000 metres, covering 141,903 hectares. We did not verify which of the nine areas includes Seiser Alm, so we do not claim it.

## Mountain huts: what you will find in late November

The huts on Seiser Alm are family-run, with table service, reachable on foot, on skis, by sled or by horse-drawn sleigh. It is the best part of the day, but it depends on the calendar. On 24 November 2026, on the first page of the official list (12 places), only two were marked open, eight closed and two had no indication. The full list, with an "open on" filter and a button to print those open today, counts 65 businesses. The site speaks of about 50 alpine huts and farms in winter, but until the season starts do not take it for granted that a stop is open: we name none, because they need to be checked day by day.

## When the weather changes: plan B

The official site invites you to check the forecast before every winter walk; we found no published rule on gondola closures for wind or bad weather, so we cannot tell you when it stops. Plan B is therefore simple. If the morning looks doubtful, check the lift status and the weather on the site before leaving Seis. If you stay in the valley, the official offer mentions for Kastelruth, Seis, Völs and Tiers winter walking routes, ice rinks and events; we did not read their hours or prices, so we give none. If you do go up and the weather turns, come down in good time: the last opening hour is 6 pm, but with darkness and snow on the path it is better to go down much earlier.

The most useful plan B is to move the day. With little snow or trails not yet packed, the plateau in late November is not at its best; from early December, when gondola, slopes and huts are running, the best day is a clear one. On 24 November 2026 the site showed 25 cm of snow at altitude and 20 in the valley, with the last snowfall on 21 November; the Sellaronda was shown as closed.

## How much to spend for a day

For an adult the certain part is the gondola: 31 € return. If you drive, remember parking: free at the valley station, 15 € or 31 € a day on the plateau (4 December to 4 April period). We did not read the prices of meals, drinks, sled rental or horse-drawn sleighs, so we do not estimate them. These are indicative averages for the verified services only.

## Common mistakes

- Planning the gondola before 4 December 2026: it is closed.
- Counting on a mountain hut being open in late November without checking its status on the site.
- Driving up during the closed hours (9:00 am to 5:00 pm with the gondola running) or without booking parking.
- Walking on cross-country or ski tracks: winter trails are separate, and those tracks are off-limits to walkers.
- Buying the Combi Card for a single day: it costs 73 €, more than the 31 € ticket.
- Trusting a single page: the site still carries last season's dates in places.
