Turin in the rain is not a city to put off: it is a city built for bad-weather days. Three museums of international standing, a square with a royal palace, and a historic-café tradition with a very particular identity. The real problem is not the weather, it is the calendar: each museum closes on a different day, and if you find out on arrival, the whole plan falls apart.

> **In short.** What to do in Turin when it rains: the Egyptian Museum in the morning (online ticket mandatory), a historic café around midday, the Royal Museums or the Mole Antonelliana in the afternoon. Watch the closing days: Royal Museums closed on Wednesday, Cinema Museum closed on Tuesday, Egyptian Museum open only until 2 pm on Monday. Thursday to Sunday is when everything works.

:::foto torino-mole | The Mole Antonelliana at dusk, with the snow-capped Alps behind (photo taken on a clear evening: in the rain it will look different)

## Which day is the right one for a rainy day?

Before choosing the stops, choose the day, because this is where Plan B is saved or spoiled. According to the official pages we read, the Royal Museums are open Thursday to Tuesday, 9:00 to 19:00 with last entry at 18:00, and closed on Wednesday. The National Museum of Cinema, inside the Mole Antonelliana, is open every day from 9:00 to 19:00 except Tuesday, when it is closed. The Egyptian Museum, finally, opens only from 9:00 to 14:00 on Monday; from Tuesday to Friday and on Sunday it is open 9:00 to 18:30, and on Saturday 9:00 to 20:00.

Put those together and on Monday you lose half a day of the Egyptian Museum, on Tuesday you lose the Mole, and on Wednesday you lose the Royal Museums. Thursday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday let you combine any of them. If you land on a Monday or Wednesday the plan still holds: just change the pair of museums.

## 1. Egyptian Museum: in the morning, with the ticket already in your pocket {coperto}

It is the stop that copes best with rain, because it is big and takes time: the Musei di Torino portal says a self-guided visit lasts two and a half hours on average. The museum was founded in 1824 and, according to the same page, welcomed 1,273,354 visitors in 2025, a good reason not to turn up without a ticket. The official site is clear: tickets are sold online only.

On price the sources do not agree, and it helps to know. The Musei di Torino portal, updated on 30 September 2026, gives 14 € full price and 28 € for the family ticket (two adults and two minors). The official tariff page, however, shows the table as an image, which cannot be read as text, and in a promotion for holders of the "Tesori dei Faraoni" exhibition ticket it mentions a reduced ticket at 15 € "instead of 18 €" and a family ticket at 30 € "instead of 36 €". The portal is not the museum's own site, so for the price trust the online checkout on the official site. We do not know which of the two figures is in force today, and we will not pick one for you.

A useful note for November: the opening-hours page lists extended openings from 9:00 to 22:00 on 23, 24, 25, 26, 28 and 29 November 2026. If you are there on those days, a rainy evening becomes a good opportunity.

## 2. A historic café around midday {coperto}

The Turismo Torino site presents Turin's historic cafés as part of the city's history: its page lists, among others, Caffè Al Bicerin, Baratti & Milano, Caffè Elena, Caffè Fiorio, Mulassano, Platti, Caffè San Carlo and Pepino. The same site warns that only venues that registered appear and that the owners entered the information themselves: it is a list, not a ranking. We did not verify opening hours or prices for these cafés, so we do not write them.

Two venues we read carefully on their official pages. Caffè Al Bicerin, at Piazza della Consolata 5, dates from 1763 and is described as one of Italy's best-known historic venues because it keeps its original furnishings; it is where the bicerin was born, the Turin drink that, according to the page, combines bitter cocoa, coffee and cream. The venue's own site speaks of "a story of 263 years" and of an original recipe that stays secret. Caffè Mulassano, at Piazza Castello 15, moved in 1907 to the square where it still stands; it is small, about thirty-one square metres, with wood, bronze and brass panelling. Inside there is a plaque crediting Angela Demichelis Nebiolo with inventing the tramezzino in 1926.

:::foto torino-mulassano | The sign and window of Caffè Mulassano at Piazza Castello 15, Turin

The page also mentions the Mulassano's best-known offer: among more than thirty fillings, the most requested are lobster, truffle and bagna cauda. Prices were not shown: we did not read them.

## 3. Royal Museums, or the Mole: the afternoon choice {coperto}

If you have only one afternoon, choose one of the two. The **Royal Museums** are a big complex: the official page mentions the Royal Palace, the Savoy Gallery, the Museum of Antiquities, the Royal Library and the Royal Gardens, with a ticket valid for the whole day. They open 9:00 to 19:00, with last entry at 18:00. The full ticket is 15 €, the reduced ticket 2 € (ages 18 to 25), and the Open ticket, valid until 31 December, 18 €. Under-18s get in free, and on the first Sunday of the month entry is free but, the site notes, it cannot be booked. From Porta Nuova station the page gives 1.4 km on foot, about 20 minutes; from Porta Susa 1.7 km, about 25 minutes.

The **Mole Antonelliana** houses the National Museum of Cinema. Hours: 9:00 to 19:00 every day except Tuesday, with the ticket office open until one hour before closing. There is a pricing detail to know: from 7 October to 8 December the museum-only ticket is reduced for everyone at 16 € (instead of 18 €) and the museum plus panoramic lift ticket at 20 € (instead of 23 €), because some exhibition areas are being set up for a new show. The pages we read add another useful note: from 9 to 14 December 2026 the museum is closed. On booking, the page recommends doing it online, especially on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays.

For the panoramic lift the official pages disagree on a detail: one table says the few extra tickets go online 19 hours before, another says 24 hours before. Check the time on the site when you book. On how the lift works in the rain we found nothing on the official pages: ask at the ticket office.

## 4. A stretch outdoors, if it stops raining {aperto}

When the rain stops, the Royal Gardens are the nearest break from the museums. Entry is free from Thursday to Tuesday, and on the winter timetable in force from Sunday 25 October 2026 to Saturday 27 March 2027 they are open 8:30 to 17:00, with last entry at 16:40. On Wednesday they are closed, like the museum. The official page also mentions a Caffè Reale inside the complex, but we did not find its hours: ask at the ticket office.

## How to order the day

The real constraint is museum closing time. An order that holds up in the rain:

:::tappe
- 9:00 am | Egyptian Museum | Opens at 9:00; on Monday it closes at 2 pm, so go early. Allow about two and a half hours.
- 12:30 pm | Break in a historic café | Mulassano is on Piazza Castello; Al Bicerin is on Piazza della Consolata. Hours not verified: check first.
- 2:00 pm | Royal Museums | Enter by 6 pm, last entry. Closed on Wednesday.
- Alternative | Mole Antonelliana | Ticket office open until one hour before the 7 pm closing. Closed on Tuesday.
:::

:::consiglio Buy the Egyptian Museum ticket online before you leave the hotel and keep the afternoon flexible: if the rain gives you an hour of clear sky, go to the Royal Gardens or ride the Mole's lift; if it keeps going, stay under the roofs of the Royal Museums.

## How much does it cost, on average

Adding up the prices we read, the Egyptian Museum (14 € according to the portal, or 18 € if the figure implied by the official promotion applies) and the Royal Museums (15 €) come to 29-33 € per person. Adding the Cinema Museum in the Mole costs 16 € until 8 December (20 € with the lift), 18 € (23 €) afterwards. These are indicative averages, without transport or meals.

## Common mistakes

- Turning up at the Egyptian Museum without an online ticket: entry is allowed only to those who bought it on the portal.
- Planning the Royal Museums on a Wednesday or the Mole on a Tuesday.
- Choosing Monday for the Egyptian Museum without knowing it closes at 2 pm.
- Counting on arriving at the Royal Museums at 6:30 pm: last entry is at 6 pm.
- Putting three museums in a single day: two are enough.

## What to skip

If you have only one rainy day, skip the museum you have not booked and keep the Egyptian Museum plus a second one. If you are in the city from 9 to 14 December, the Mole is closed: fall back on the Royal Museums.
