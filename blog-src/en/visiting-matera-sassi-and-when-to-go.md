You do not visit Matera with a map in your hand; you visit it with your feet. The Sassi are a city carved and built on the edge of a canyon, and the best way to understand them is to walk, climb, descend and pause in the few places you can really go inside. This guide puts one day in order, explains when to go and says plainly where the sources disagree.

> **In short.** The Sassi of Matera are best seen on foot in a day: start in Sasso Barisano, climb to the Civita and the Cathedral, then descend into Sasso Caveoso and the Casa Grotta di Vico Solitario. Spring and autumn are named as the best seasons. Tickets: the Casa Grotta costs 8 €, the Palombaro Lungo 3 €, the Crypt of Original Sin needs an online booking, and since 2026 the Murgia Timone viewpoint is ticketed too (7 € from November to March). Bring proper shoes and do not count on a winter sunset up there.

## What the Sassi are, in a few lines

According to the UNESCO entry, the property of the Sassi and the Park of the Rupestrian Churches comprises houses, churches, monasteries and hermitages built into the natural caves of the Murgia, covering 1,016 hectares, with more than a thousand dwellings and many workshops. Occupation goes back to the Palaeolithic and intensifies from the 8th century. There are two Sassi, Barisano and Caveoso, and between them lies the Civita, the first inhabited nucleus, where a Romanesque cathedral was built in the 13th century. Italia.it notes that the site has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1993. The UNESCO entry adds that the entire population of the Sassi was relocated in the 1950s, and that people began to return in the 1980s.

On the date of the evacuation the sources wander: the Ministry of Culture mentions a clearance law of 1952, Lonely Planet Italia says people lived in the Casa Grotta "until 1956", and Italia.it says 1958. UNESCO says only "the 1950s". When you read a precise date, ask where it comes from.

## How to get to Matera

The nearest airport is Bari Palese, 55 kilometres away according to the Comune's tourist portal, which also lists Brindisi (140 km) and Naples (250 km) and a shuttle between Bari Airport and Matera. The shuttle timetable on the portal is dated, so check it before you travel.

Trains hold a surprise. The nearest station on the national network, the same source says, is Ferrandina-Scalo Matera, and from there you continue by road. The direct option from Bari is the FAL (Ferrovie Appulo Lucane) train from Bari Centrale: it stops at Matera Centrale, Matera Sud and Matera Villa Longo. Careful: the FAL station is not inside Bari Centrale; its entrance is at Corso Italia 8. We found no official journey time or frequency, so we do not quote any.

## When to go: climate and seasons

Lonely Planet Italia names spring and autumn as the best seasons, with highs between 15 and 20 °C and little rain, and says winter lets you see the Casa Grotta, the Palombaro Lungo and Santa Maria de Idris without the crowds of the spring bank holidays and summer. Snow, per the same page, usually does not arrive before mid-February, and from December to February highs sit around 9-10 °C.

Here the sources disagree. Lonely Planet writes that winter rain is "practically nil", whereas the Climi e Viaggi climate page makes December the wettest month, with 75 mm over 9 days (November 60 mm over 7 days, October 45 mm over 5). The same page gives average highs of 31 °C in July and 30 in August, and 20 °C in October. In short: summer is hot and busier, spring and autumn are good for walking, winter is quieter but you want a waterproof jacket.

## Before you go: shoes, slopes and hours

The lanes of the Civita, the Comune writes, can only be walked. Paving and steps are part of the route: shoes with a grippy sole, not sandals. Hours change with the season and we are now in autumn: the Casa Grotta, for example, from October to December opens Monday to Friday from 9:30 to 18:00, with last access at 17:40, and Saturday and Sunday until 19:00, with last access at 18:40. The site adds that you can enter up to 20 minutes before closing and that, with at least one working day's notice, you can ask to visit outside the scheduled hours.

## Morning: Sasso Barisano and the Civita

The Comune di Matera suggests a walking route that starts at the Villa Comunale and goes down via Pentasuglia to via D'Addozio, already inside Sasso Barisano. Here you meet the Monastery of Sant'Agostino, built in 1591 and partly rebuilt in 1747, whose church stands on the ruins of a rock-cut monastery; from the churchyard the view opens onto Murgia Timone and the Gravina canyon. A little further on, the church of San Pietro Barisano has an 18th-century facade over an underground church; according to the Comune's page, last updated in January 2024, the interior is not open to visitors.

From via Fiorentini you climb the Civita, the old heart dominated by the Cathedral. According to Italia.it the Cathedral, a 13th-century Apulian Romanesque church, has a sixteen-ray rose window, a 16th-century limestone nativity scene and a Byzantine fresco of 1270, and the square in front gives a view over the Sassi. We found no hours or admission terms on an official page: check on the spot.

## Lunch break: what to taste

Italia.it points to three dishes to start with: cialledda (bread soaked with tomato, garlic and olives, hot with egg and bay leaf or cold), Senise crusco peppers, and pignata, mutton slow-cooked in a clay pot with potatoes, tomatoes and soppressata. We name no restaurants because we have not verified any.

## Early afternoon: Sasso Caveoso

From the Civita you descend towards via Madonna delle Virtù, which runs along the edge of the ravine, to the rock-cut complex of Madonna delle Virtù and San Nicola dei Greci, founded between the 10th and 11th centuries, which hosts sculpture exhibitions from July to October. Then you reach Piazza San Pietro Caveoso, with the church rebuilt in 1706 and still used for worship, and Vico Solitario.

The Storica Casa Grotta di Vico Solitario is the clearest place to understand how people lived: the site gives admission at 8 € full and 5 € reduced, including the Neviera, a natural cave and the rock church of Sant'Agostino al Casalnuovo, plus a multilingual audio guide. The Ministry of Culture listing, updated in 2020 and with inconsistent hours on the same page, gives instead 3 € and 2 €: we would not use it as a reference. According to the ministerial entry, the house preserves the hearth, the bed, the stall with the mule's manger and the rainwater collection system, with its channelling and cistern.

## End of the day: the viewpoints

For the classic view of the stone nativity scene, Italia.it recommends the Belvedere of Murgia Timone, on the opposite side of the ravine, early in the morning or at sunset. The Comune also mentions a Belvedere square reachable by car from the SS 7 Appia towards Laterza (we did not verify whether access is regulated). Piazzetta Pascoli, in the centre, is a balcony over the Sassi without going far (the Comune mentions no ticket).

## Is the Murgia Timone viewpoint paid? What changed in 2026

This is where our October guide needed updating. The visitor site of the Parco della Murgia Materana (run by Parco Sassi, in agreement with the Park Authority) sells an access ticket for the Murgia Timone area: 10 € from 1 April to 31 October and 7 € from 1 November to 31 March, with a 50% reduction under 25 and free entry for residents of Matera and Montescaglioso and for accompanied children under 6. It includes the area's trail network, the rock church of San Falcione, the Jazzo Gattini visitor centre, toilets and the shuttle to and from the viewpoint. One discrepancy to flag: on the same site the booking form also shows a 5 € item for a free winter/autumn visit and a different base price, so the final figure should be checked when you buy.

The Park's hours, per the same page, are 9:00 to 18:00 from 16 September to 30 November and 10:00 to 16:30 from 1 December to the end of February. That means the sunset at the viewpoint that Italia.it suggests has to be fitted around the closing time: check the day's sunset and the closing hour before you go up. The safest idea in this season is to do Murgia Timone in the morning and keep Piazzetta Pascoli, for which we found no ticket or hours, for the late afternoon.

If you prefer to walk there, take the Sentiero 406 from Porta Pistola, on via Madonna delle Virtù. It drops to the ravine, crosses a suspension bridge of about 70 metres and climbs onto the plateau. The Park's site rates it medium difficulty, lists 2 hours as the average visit time and a ticket of 10 € from November to March (15 € from April to October), which also covers the Murgia Timone services. Trekking shoes are compulsory: with flip-flops, sandals or slippers you are not let in. The route takes more time and breath than it looks from below: do not leave it for the end of the day with tired legs.

## If you have a second day

Three ideas with one certain fact each. The Crypt of Original Sin, which Italia.it lists among the rock churches not to be missed, dates from the Lombard period; it can only be visited by online booking, and in October the slots start at 9:30, 11:00, 12:30, 15:30 and 17:00, reduced to four (without the 17:00) from November to February. The ticket office is at the Azienda Agricola Dragone and you must arrive half an hour before. The Palombaro Lungo, under Piazza Vittorio Veneto, is a cistern dug in phases from the 16th century, 16 metres deep, 50 long and holding 5 million litres according to the official site. It is open every day from 10:00 to 13:00 and from 15:00 to 18:00, with no booking, as a self-guided visit of about 15 minutes with a leaflet in several languages; if it is crowded you may wait a few minutes. Adults pay 3 € and minors go in free. Casa Noha, a short step from the Cathedral, is a project curated by FAI per Italia.it; we did not verify hours or prices.

## How much it costs, on average

The figures we verified on official sites, all to be read as indicative averages: Casa Grotta 8 € full and 5 € reduced; Palombaro Lungo 3 € for adults; Murgia Timone area 7 € from November to March (10 € from April to October); Sentiero 406 10 € from November to March (15 € from April to October). For walking through the Sassi along the Comune's route we found no entry ticket on the pages we read. We did not read the price of the Crypt of Original Sin or of the museums, so we do not quantify them. Meals and transport were not verified.

A sample sum, purely indicative and with the fares above: the Casa Grotta plus the Palombaro Lungo come to 11 € per full-price person; add Murgia Timone in low season and you reach 18 €, not counting the Crypt.

## The questions people really ask

**Do I need to book?** Only for the Crypt of Original Sin, which can only be visited online. The Casa Grotta and the Palombaro Lungo are paid at the desk (the Palombaro states outright that there is no booking); for Murgia Timone and the Sentiero 406 the Park's site has a purchase page with a calendar.

**What can I do if it rains?** Three indoor visits with verified details: the Casa Grotta, the Palombaro Lungo and the Crypt (the latter by booking). The Comune notes that the lanes of the Civita can only be walked, so wear shoes with a grippy sole and a waterproof; for Murgia Timone the Park recommends checking the forecast before your visit.

**With children?** Verified details: at the Casa Grotta entry is free up to and including age ten and reduced to 3 € from 11 to 18; the Palombaro Lungo is free for minors; at Murgia Timone children under 6 are free when accompanied by a parent. We did not verify how accessible the routes are with a pushchair: the lanes are steps and slopes.

**Can I do it all in one day?** As an editorial estimate, one full day is enough for the two Sassi with one or two indoor visits. With Murgia Timone and the Crypt you need two days, also because the Park's hours and the Casa Grotta's overlap in the afternoon.

## Common mistakes

- Turning up at the Crypt without a booking: it is online only.
- Arriving at the last minute: you need to be there half an hour before the slot.
- Trusting a single listing for Casa Grotta prices: three sources, three figures.
- Assuming the Murgia Timone viewpoint is free: there is now a ticket and a closing time.
- Turning up at the Sentiero 406 in light shoes: without suitable footwear you are not let in.
- Counting on sunset at Murgia Timone after 1 December, when the Park closes at 16:30.
- Climbing up and down without proper shoes.
- Planning everything on summer hours in autumn.

## What to skip

With only half a day, skip the stops that require booking and keep the Comune's walk with a single visit, the Casa Grotta. The viewpoints cost nothing and remain the best moment.
