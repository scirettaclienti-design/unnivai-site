Eating in Catania is not solved with a restaurant: it is solved with a well-built day. You start with a granita, pass through the market, have something fried for lunch and finish with a pasta that here has an opera-house name. This guide puts the tastings in the order that makes sense.

> **In short.** Morning: granita and brioche. Mid-morning: fish market and street food. Lunch: arancino and pasta alla Norma. Afternoon: sweets. Evening: horse meat or fish, if you like. Prices are indicative averages.

:::tappe
- Morning | Granita with brioche | Almond, pistachio, coffee or lemon, with Sicilian brioche. About €4.
- Mid-morning | Fish market and surroundings | Watch the market and taste standing up.
- Lunch | Arancino and pasta alla Norma | In Catania the fried one is masculine; Norma is the first course not to skip.
- Afternoon | Cannolo and sweets | Sheep's ricotta and marzipan.
- Evening | Horse meat or fish | One choice, not both.
:::

## 1. Granita and brioche

The Catanese breakfast is granita (water, sugar and fruit) with a Sicilian brioche, leavened with eggs and scented with vanilla or citrus. The flavours to start from are almond, pistachio, coffee and lemon. If you are short on time, pick just one: coffee granita with cream is the most recognisable. Indicative average cost: about €4.

## 2. Seltz with lemon and salt

At the city's kiosks they drink a seltz made with lemon juice, sparkling water, salt and a pinch of bicarbonate. It sounds odd until it is hot. About €2.

## 3. The fish market and its street food

The fish market, which locals call "a piscaria", is a few steps from Piazza Duomo, near the Fountain of the Seven Channels. You do not have to buy anything: just walking through it is a taste of the city. Around it you find kiosks and small places selling food to eat standing up. For hours, practical tips and what to avoid, read the guide to the [Catania fish market: hours and how to visit](/blog/catania-fish-market-hours-and-tips).

## 4. Arancino (masculine)

In Catania it is arancino, and it has the cone shape that recalls Etna: rice with ragù, breaded and fried. It costs around €2 in bars and simple rosticcerie. If you want a serious comparison, taste the classic ragù one before trying variations.

## 5. Pasta alla Norma

Pasta with tomato sauce, fried aubergine, salted ricotta and basil. The name recalls Bellini's *Norma*; he was born in Catania. It is the first course that best represents the city. In simple trattorie it costs around €8; in smarter places it goes up.

## 6. Spaghetti alla carrettiera

Oil, raw garlic, pepper and grated pecorino, with some variants adding tomato. It is the right choice if you have already had the Norma and want a second pasta, drier.

## 7. Horse meat

It is a true Catanese speciality, not a curiosity for tourists: meatballs, sausages and steaks. The best-known street to try it is Via del Plebiscito. As a main course you spend around €10. If it is not for you, there is no obligation.

## 8. Swordfish

Usually grilled with a drizzle of oil. It is the dish to order if you want fish in the evening, usually from €15 up as a main course. Always ask what is fresh that day.

## 9. Cannolo

A crisp shell with sheep's ricotta cream. The practical advice is to eat it filled to order: a cannolo that has been filled for hours loses its crunch, which is half the pleasure. About €2 in bars.

## 10. Sant'Agata sweets

The minnuzze di Sant'Agata are small cassatine with sponge cake, ricotta, marzipan and a candied cherry, and the olivette are almond-paste sweets shaped like olives. They are found mostly in January and February, around the saint's feast on 3-5 February. Out of season do not expect them everywhere.

:::consiglio Do not do it all in one day. Pick four tastings and walk between them: in Catania the centre is compact and walking is easy.
