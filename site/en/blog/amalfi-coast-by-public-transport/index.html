How to get around the Amalfi Coast without a car

Amalfi Coast9 minby Ivano ScirettaChecked on November 16, 2026

Quick answer

To get around the Amalfi Coast without a car you need SITA buses (COSTIERASITA ticket, 10 € for 24 hours) and, when the sea allows, Travelmar ferries: Salerno-Amalfi 12 €, Amalfi-Positano 10 €. The SITA winter timetable is valid from 2 November 2026 to 21 March 2027; rough seas can suspend the ferries, so always keep the bus as plan B.

Area
Amalfi Coast, Campania: from Salerno to Positano via Amalfi

The Amalfi Coast works well without a car: SITA buses and ferries cover the main towns, as long as you know the winter timetables, which changed on 2 November. Here are two days with the transfers explained, the costs read on official websites, and the things that decide whether a ferry day actually happens.

  1. Day 1 · morning
    Salerno to Amalfi

    Travelmar ferry (35 minutes according to the timetable page) or a SITA bus along the coast.

  2. Day 1 · afternoon
    Amalfi and Atrani

    On foot in Amalfi, with the SITA bus to Atrani.

  3. Day 2 · morning
    Positano or the Path of the Gods

    SITA bus or ferry to Positano; or a bus from Amalfi to Bomerano for the trail.

  4. Day 2 · afternoon
    Back

    Ferry or bus from Positano to Amalfi, then on towards Salerno.

Tip

The sea decides more than the timetable does. On the day we read the Travelmar website (16 November 2026) a notice showed services suspended because of the weather: if your plan depends on a ferry, keep the SITA bus ready and check the notice the same morning, not the evening before.

Which day do you need?

How public transport works on the coast#

Two operators cover almost everything. SITA Sud, a bus company, links the coast and the valleys with several lines: Amalfi-Positano-Sorrento, Amalfi-Maiori-Salerno, Amalfi-Agerola, Scala-Ravello-Amalfi and Maiori-Tramonti. Travelmar runs fast ferries between Salerno, Amalfi, Positano and, according to its website, also Cetara and Maiori. We did not find a joint ticket between the two: they are separate tickets.

UNESCO, which inscribed the coast on the World Heritage List in 1997, lists among the threats to the area the pressure of heavy tourism in some urban zones, mainly due to vehicle traffic. It is a good reason to leave the car at home.

SITA bus timetable in winter#

SITA published a notice on 29 October 2026: from 2 November the timetables of Amalfi-Positano-Sorrento, Amalfi-Maiori-Salerno, Amalfi-Agerola, Scala-Ravello-Amalfi and Maiori-Tramonti changed. The winter timetable PDF states it is valid from 2 November 2026 to 21 March 2027. That is the one to trust, not the old autumn tables (14 September to 1 November) that still circulate on some third-party sites.

In the PDF each run carries a letter: F for working days Monday to Saturday, H for Sundays and holidays, G for daily runs, L for Monday to Friday. A few first runs: the first daily Amalfi-Positano bus leaves Amalfi at 6:20 and reaches Positano (Sponda stop) at 7:00, about 40 minutes. The first working-day Amalfi-Maiori-Salerno run leaves Amalfi at 5:15 and calls at Atrani, Minori, Maiori and Cetara. These are single runs used as examples, not the frequency, which we did not compute: open the PDF or the Moovit, Google Maps or UnicoCampania apps, which SITA lists as ways to check the timetable.

The COSTIERASITA ticket: 10 € for 24 hours#

According to the SITA Sud website, the COSTIERASITA ticket costs 10 € and is valid for 24 hours from first validation, with unlimited trips on SITA Sud vehicles in these municipalities: Agerola, Amalfi, Atrani, Cetara, Conca dei Marini, Furore, Maiori, Massa Lubrense, Meta, Minori, Positano, Piano di Sorrento, Praiano, Ravello, Salerno, Sant'Agnello, Scala, Sorrento, Tramonti and Vietri sul Mare. You validate it on first use, by hand if the machine is missing or broken, and it is personal: write your name and date of birth in pen.

It is sold, among other places, at the via Vinciprova ticket office in Salerno (open on working days from 6:45 to 18:45) and at authorised points of sale listed on the Unico Campania website. We did not read the single-ride price on an official page, so we cannot say how many rides make the day ticket worth it: do the maths before you buy.

Day 1: Salerno, Amalfi and Atrani#

From Salerno to Amalfi you have two choices. The Travelmar ferry, according to its timetable page, takes 35 minutes and costs 12 €; the winter departures we read leave from Piazza Concordia at 9:00 and 9:40 (via Cetara and Maiori, 60 minutes), 11:10 and 14:10. The SITA Amalfi-Maiori-Salerno bus runs from the early morning (the first working-day run leaves Amalfi at 5:15) and does not depend on the sea.

In Amalfi the natural stop is the Cathedral, in the middle of town. We did not read opening hours or entry prices on an official page, so we do not give them: check before you climb the steps.

Atrani is the next stop after Amalfi on the SITA line to Salerno: on the winter 5:15 run from Amalfi, the bus is at Atrani at 5:20, five minutes later. In the photo below you can see the road on its arches above the beach.

Day 2: Positano or the Path of the Gods#

From Amalfi to Positano there are two ways. The SITA Amalfi-Positano-Sorrento bus takes about 40 minutes on the 6:20 run we read in the timetable (it reaches Positano, Sponda stop, at 7:00). The Travelmar ferry, according to its timetable page, takes 25 minutes and costs 10 €, with departures from Amalfi at 10:40, 12:00 and 15:00 and from Positano at 12:10, 13:30 and 15:30. In Positano we give no visiting hours or restaurants, because we have not verified them.

If you would rather walk: the Path of the Gods. The Monti Lattari Regional Park rates it easy (E), with some exposed stretches, 638 metres of total ascent and descent and about 4 hours of walking. It starts from Piazza Capasso in Bomerano, a hamlet of Agerola, and reaches Nocelle after about 4.5 km; from there you can go down the roughly 1,700 steps to Arienzo on the main road in about an hour, then walk to Positano (10 minutes) or take a SITA bus. The Park mentions a spring at Grotta Biscotto, near the start, and a fountain at Nocelle: bring a large bottle anyway.

To reach Bomerano without a car there are the SITA buses of the Amalfi-Agerola line (the Park's website mentions frequent runs from Amalfi), or the shuttle combined with the Travelmar ferries: according to the dedicated page, from Salerno the 8:40 ferry reaches Amalfi at 9:15 and the 9:30 shuttle reaches Bomerano at 10:10, for 18 € in all; from Positano the departures are at 10:00, 15:30, 17:00 and 18:30 and cost 16 €. The page does not say which period these times apply to, so check at the Travelmar ticket office before relying on them.

Travelmar ferries in winter: what we read#

The Travelmar timetable page, read on 16 November 2026, shows the current timetable with fewer runs than in summer. From Salerno to Amalfi 9:00, 9:40, 11:10, 14:10 (12 €, 35 minutes, or 60 via Cetara and Maiori); from Salerno to Positano 9:40, 11:10, 14:10 (17 €, 70 minutes, or 80 via Cetara and Maiori). From Amalfi to Salerno 10:35, 13:00, 14:15 and 16:30. The same page says links with Minori and Vietri sul Mare are available from April 2027, so for now the bus is the only option to those two towns. If you want to return to Salerno in the evening, the ferry leaves you stranded early: the last departure from Amalfi that we read is at 16:30.

Arriving from the Sorrento peninsula#

If you come from Naples, the common route is the Circumvesuviana train to Sorrento and then a SITA bus to Positano and Amalfi. For this stretch we only have a third-party source, Sorrento Insider, which gives 1 hour 15 minutes of travel and two different ticket prices (3.60 € in one place, 4.60 € in another on the same page): we do not state a price, and for the official timetable and fare it is best to open the operator's website, EAV.

How much to spend (indicative averages)#

For two days: COSTIERASITA bus ticket 10 € a day (20 € over two days), one Salerno-Amalfi ferry at 12 € and one Amalfi-Positano ferry at 10 €. With the Path of the Gods combined ticket (shuttle plus ferry from Salerno) you pay 18 €. For one person that is about 42 € of transport (20 + 12 + 10), not counting entry fees or meals. These are indicative averages, not a promise: prices change with the season.

Common mistakes on the coast without a car#

  • Relying on one ferry: if the sea is rough, services stop. Keep the bus as plan B.
  • Using an autumn SITA table: the ones from 2 November apply.
  • Forgetting to validate the COSTIERASITA ticket on first use and to write your name on it.
  • Planning Minori or Vietri sul Mare by boat: the link returns in April 2027.
  • Starting the Path of the Gods late: the Park indicates about 4 hours of walking, plus the return, so start in the morning.

Plan B: if the weather changes#

When the sea is rough the ferries stop but the SITA buses are the alternative: Amalfi-Positano can also be done by bus. If it rains, postpone the Path of the Gods, which the Park describes as having exposed stretches without protection.

What to leave out#

  • Positano, Amalfi, Ravello and Atrani in a single day: that is four places, and with winter timetables you cannot fit them without rushing.
  • The car: along the coast road it does not pay off, and this plan does not need it.
  • Places we do not know: we name none, because we have not verified them.

Frequently asked questions

Can you get around the Amalfi Coast without a car?

Yes. SITA Sud runs bus lines linking Sorrento, Positano, Amalfi, Maiori and Salerno, and in winter Travelmar ferries connect Salerno, Amalfi, Positano and some towns in between. The COSTIERASITA ticket at 10 € is valid for 24 hours on all SITA buses in the coastal municipalities.

How much is the COSTIERASITA ticket and where do you buy it?

According to the SITA Sud website it costs 10 €, is valid for 24 hours from first validation and allows unlimited trips in the listed municipalities (including Amalfi, Positano, Maiori, Ravello and Salerno). It is sold, among other places, at the ticket office on via Vinciprova in Salerno and at Unico Campania points of sale; you must validate it on first use and it is personal.

Do the ferries run in winter?

The Travelmar website says the service is available all year, but with fewer departures than in summer: on 16 November 2026, for example, the page showed services suspended because of the sea conditions. According to the same page, links with Minori and Vietri sul Mare return from April 2027.

How do you reach the Path of the Gods without a car?

By SITA bus from Amalfi towards Agerola, to Bomerano where the trail starts, or by the shuttle combined with the Travelmar ferry (18 € from Salerno, 16 € from Positano). From the end point, Nocelle, about 1,700 steps lead down to Arienzo, and from there you walk to Positano or take a SITA bus.

When do the SITA bus timetables change?

The winter timetable is valid from 2 November 2026 to 21 March 2027. The SITA website has published two timetable changes in two months (14 September and 2 November): always check the latest PDF before you travel.
