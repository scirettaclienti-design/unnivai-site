Bologna in a day: tortellini, porticoes and a walking plan

Bologna9 minby Ivano ScirettaChecked on October 30, 2026

Quick answer

Morning around Piazza Maggiore, San Petronio (free entry) and the Clock Tower; lunch in the Quadrilatero; early afternoon at the Archiginnasio; if you have energy left, the San Luca portico. When we opened the official booking page, the Asinelli Tower was listed as closed for maintenance: the Clock Tower is the alternative it points to. The art collections are closed on Mondays.

Area
Historic centre of Bologna, Emilia-Romagna: from Piazza Maggiore to the Quadrilatero and the Archiginnasio
Duration
One day on foot, almost entirely under the porticoes
Indicative budget
About 25 € in tickets for the Clock Tower with collections (10 €), the Archiginnasio with audioguide (10 €) and the paid route in San Petronio (5 €), meals excluded (indicative averages)
Getting around
On foot; for San Luca you walk under the portico or take the buses listed by Bologna Welcome
To check
Asinelli Tower closed for maintenance, winter hours, Monday closure of the art collections

--- Bologna is walked with a roof over your head. The porticoes work as a corridor between stops and, if the weather turns, the plan barely changes. This itinerary puts one walking day in order across square, towers, market and porticoes, with tortellini at the centre, and says where hours are seasonal, where sources disagree and what is closed right now.

  1. Morning
    Piazza Maggiore and San Petronio

    Free entry to the basilica, no reservation. The paid route through three chapels costs 5 €.

  2. 10:00 am
    Clock Tower

    Reservation required; from September to February the first entry is at 10:00 am.

  3. Lunch
    Quadrilatero

    Via Clavature, Drapperie, Pescherie Vecchie: cured meats, fresh pasta and stalls to eat at.

  4. Afternoon
    Archiginnasio and Anatomical Theatre

    The old university seat: audioguide visit, entries every 10 minutes.

  5. Late
    San Luca portico

    Optional, uphill: more than 2 km of slope or steps.

Try it

What is closed or changes in autumn?Outdoors#

Three things to know before you go. First, the Asinelli Tower. Bologna Welcome's booking page carried the notice "Torre Asinelli chiusa per manutenzione nell'area", with a pointer to the Clock Tower. When it reopens, the climb needs a reservation, has 498 steps and no lift. Second, the Clock Tower has seasonal hours. From 7 September 2026 to 28 February 2027 the first entry is at 10:00 am and the last at 6:20 pm, with three entries an hour. Third, the Municipal Art Collections in the same palace have different hours (Tuesday and Thursday 2-7 pm, Wednesday and Friday 10 am-7 pm, Saturday, Sunday and holidays 10 am-6:30 pm) and are closed on non-holiday Mondays.

The practical consequence is simple: if Monday is all you have, the tower works but the collections do not, and the Asinelli needs checking. If you have a choice, pick a Tuesday to Friday.

Morning: Piazza Maggiore and San PetronioOutdoors#

Start in the square where everything converges. The Basilica of San Petronio, according to its official site, has free entry and needs no reservation for individual visits. To enter the chapels of the Magi (Bolognini), San Sebastiano and San Vincenzo Ferrer you buy the paid route on the spot: 5 € full and 3 € reduced, from 10:00 am to 5:30 pm with last entry at 5:15 pm. Other single chapels and the Presbytery are visited only on request by email, at least a week ahead, at 3 € per person for each.

We do not give the nave's visiting hours: services change them, and the basilica's site warns of days when guided visits are cancelled for events. Check the page on the day.

Clock Tower and Palazzo d'AccursioIndoors#

It is the alternative climb to the Asinelli, and in autumn it is convenient because it opens at 10:00 am. The ticket includes the audioguide visit of the tower (about 40 minutes) and entry to the Municipal Art Collections (about an hour): 10 € full, 7 € reduced, according to Bologna Welcome. Reservation is mandatory, you show up 5 minutes before and if you are late you cannot go up on the next slot. The meeting point is the Sala Farnese, on the second floor of Palazzo d'Accursio, Piazza Maggiore 6. The summit terrace is reached by a narrow stair, with a waiver signed on site and a ban under 8 years of age; there is no lift.

Lunch in the Quadrilatero: where tortellini liveOutdoors#

The Quadrilatero is the centre's shop-and-market district: according to Emilia Romagna Tourism its perimeter is drawn by the crossing of via Rizzoli, via dell'Archiginnasio, via Farini and via Castiglione. On via Clavature there is one entrance of the Mercato di Mezzo, with stalls to eat from, take away or at shared tables; on via Capraie and via Drapperie you find delis and shops with mortadella, fresh pasta and sweets; on via Pescherie Vecchie, tigelle and cured meats for an aperitivo. The same source names the Osteria del Sole, in Vicolo dei Ranocchi, described as the oldest osteria in Bologna, where only wine is served and everyone brings their own food.

We do not name tortellini restaurants, because we read none on the sources we consulted. What we can give you is a yardstick: the Dotta Confraternita del Tortellino reports the deposit of 15 April 2008 at the Chamber of Commerce, which defines the classic shape, the filling, the thickness of the pasta and a final weight of 5 grams, with the pasta cut into squares 3 cm on a side. According to the same page, for about a thousand tortellini you need 10 eggs and a kilo of flour for the pasta; 300 g of pork loin, 300 g of raw ham, 300 g of mortadella, 450 g of Parmigiano Reggiano and 3 eggs for the filling; and for the broth a kilo of beef, half a hen, celery, carrot, onion and salt. Asking whether the broth is made in-house and whether the pasta is rolled daily is a more useful test than any ranking.

For ragù the source is Bologna Welcome, which gives the updated version deposited by the Accademia Italiana della Cucina at the Chamber of Commerce (the original dates from 1982): 300 g of minced beef (diaphragm, belly or shoulder), 150 g of pancetta, 50 g each of carrot, celery and onion, 300 g of tomato passata or peeled tomatoes, half a glass each of dry white wine and whole milk, broth, and about two hours of cooking.

Early afternoon: Archiginnasio and Anatomical TheatreIndoors#

The Archiginnasio, the old university seat, is visited with an audioguide or a guided tour. According to the Musei di Bologna page, updated in July 2025 and so worth rechecking, audioguide visits run Monday to Saturday from 10:00 am to 5:40 pm, with entries every 10 minutes and a duration of 40 minutes; the full ticket is 10 € and the reduced 8 €. Guided tours cost 12 € full and leave at fixed times (for July and August, in Italian at 1:30 and 3:30 pm). Put this stop after lunch: it is entirely indoors and takes no more than an hour.

The porticoes and the San Luca porticoIndoors#

The porticoes are not a backdrop: UNESCO inscribes them as a serial site of 12 portico ensembles, and the Comune notes that the whole network is 62 km long. The listing dates from 28 July 2021. Among the twelve ensembles is "San Luca".

The San Luca portico links the city to the sanctuary. Here the sources disagree: the Comune speaks of about 3,600 metres and notes that the traditional count of 666 arches is actually slightly lower, depending on how they are counted; Bologna Welcome gives 3.5 km and 666 arches. On the route the Comune is precise: from the Arco Bonaccorsi you follow via Saragozza, flat, for about a kilometre and a half; then for more than 2 kilometres you climb, partly on steps. Bologna Welcome says to continue under the portico from Porta Saragozza. The alternative, per Bologna Welcome: bus 20 to the church of San Martino in Casalecchio di Reno, then bus 58 to the sanctuary. We did not read walking times: our estimate is no less than a couple of hours there and back on foot, and it is only an estimate.

How much to spend (indicative averages)#

With the figures read: Clock Tower with collections 10 €, Archiginnasio with audioguide 10 €, San Petronio's paid route 5 €. In all about 25 € in tickets for one adult, with San Luca and the basilica free. These are indicative averages: they depend on discounts, days and cards such as the Bologna Welcome Card (which the sources list as free for the Asinelli and the Clock Tower).

What to leave out#

  • Too many towers: if the Asinelli is closed, the Clock Tower is enough for a panorama.
  • Mondays for museums: the art collections stay closed.
  • San Luca in the rain or at day's end if you are tired: it is uphill and best in full daylight.
  • The hunt for the "right" restaurant: the broth test and a Quadrilatero stall are better.
Tip

Book the Clock Tower for 10:00 am and do San Petronio right after: they are a few steps apart, the first runs on fixed slots and the second does not. That way you reach the Quadrilatero at lunchtime, and the Archiginnasio, with entries every 10 minutes, fits wherever it falls.

Frequently asked questions

Can you see Bologna in one day?

Yes, if you choose. Piazza Maggiore, San Petronio, the Clock Tower, the Quadrilatero and the Archiginnasio are a few minutes apart on foot. San Luca, with its portico, is a half-day addition.

Can I climb the Asinelli Tower?

When we opened Bologna Welcome's booking page, it carried the notice "Torre Asinelli chiusa per manutenzione nell'area" (closed for maintenance in the area) and pointed to the Clock Tower. When open, the climb needs a reservation, has 498 steps, no lift, and a ticket of 5 € full and 3 € reduced. Check first.

How long are Bologna's porticoes?

The network of porticoed paths is 62 km long according to the Comune. They were inscribed on the UNESCO list on 28 July 2021 as a serial site of 12 portico ensembles. The San Luca portico is given different lengths: about 3,600 metres by the Comune, 3.5 km by Bologna Welcome.

What is the deposited Bolognese tortellino?

The Dotta Confraternita del Tortellino says it deposited on 15 April 2008, at the Bologna Chamber of Commerce and by notarial deed, the recipe of the Classic Tortellino: shape, filling, thickness of the pasta and a final weight of 5 grams. It is a reference recipe, not a rule for restaurants.

What if it rains?

The plan holds: porticoes cover a good part of the walking between stops, and San Petronio, the Clock Tower with the collections and the Archiginnasio are indoors.
