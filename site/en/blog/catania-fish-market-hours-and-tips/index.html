Catania fish market: hours, what to see and how to visit

Catania6 minby Ivano ScirettaChecked on October 29, 2026

Quick answer

Go early in the morning, Monday to Saturday, in closed non-slip shoes. Give the market 30-60 minutes and, if you want to eat, another hour nearby. Sunday is closed.

Area
Historic centre, behind Piazza Duomo
Indicative hours
Monday to Saturday, from the morning to early afternoon. Closed on Sunday
Duration
30 minutes to 2 hours

The Catania fish market is not an attraction set up for tourists: it is a market that works every morning, loud, wet and beautiful. To visit it well you need a few rules: the right time, the right shoes and an idea of what to look at.

  1. Before 10
    Entrance from the Piazza Duomo side

    The stalls are fully stocked and the light is better.

  2. Mid-morning
    Walk between the stalls

    Swordfish, tuna, sea bream, sea urchins, octopus.

  3. Edges
    Fruit and vegetables

    Oranges, broccoli, artichokes: calmer.

  4. Afterwards
    Tastings standing up

    Street food nearby.

Hours: what we know and what we do not#

The sources we consulted agree on two points: the market is open Monday to Saturday and closed on Sunday. On exact hours they differ slightly: one says about 7 am to 2 pm, another 8 am to 2:30 pm. That is why the practical rule is the safest one: arrive by mid-morning. After noon some stalls are already packing up, and the best produce is gone.

When to go#

The best window is before 10: full choice and less heat, especially in summer. The busiest time is around 8:30-10:30. If you want to take photos, choose a weekday and make eye contact with the vendors before shooting.

Where it is#

In the historic centre, a few metres from Piazza Duomo and near the Fountain of the Seven Channels. From the north you can enter through Porta Uzeda, from the south through Piazza Duomo. From the central station it takes about 10 minutes on foot.

What to see#

They sell swordfish, tuna, sea bream, sea bass, red mullet, sea urchins and octopus, and by weight clams, cuttlefish and mussels. At the edges there are fruit and vegetable stalls, including Sicilian IGP blood oranges, broccoli and artichokes: they are calmer and let you breathe between one stall and the next.

What to bring and what to avoid#

  • Closed shoes with a non-slip sole. The floor is wet and slippery.
  • Strollers and wheelchairs: hard at peak hours, and there are no ramps inside.
  • Fish to cook later: risky in the heat if you cannot keep it cold.
  • Photos of vendors without asking: a nod is enough.

Where to eat nearby#

Around the market there are kiosks, bars and small trattorie, and by mid-morning street-food stands appear selling things like stigghiola and pane con la milza. For a full tasting route read what to eat in Catania.

Frequently asked questions

What are the opening hours of the Catania fish market?

The sources we consulted indicate opening Monday to Saturday and closing on Sunday, with slightly different hours (from about 7 or 8 until 2 or 2:30 pm). The practical rule is to go by mid-morning: after noon many stalls start to pack up.

Is the Catania fish market open on Sunday?

No, according to the sources consulted it is closed on Sunday.

How long does a visit take?

30 to 60 minutes to walk between the stalls; about two hours if you stop to eat nearby.

Is there an entrance fee?

No, entry is free. You only spend if you buy something.
