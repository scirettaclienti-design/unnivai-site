Milan in a weekend when it rains: a Plan B itinerary

Milan9 minby Ivano ScirettaChecked on October 30, 2026

Quick answer

On Saturday stay around Piazza Duomo: Museo del Novecento, the Cathedral and Duomo Museum, Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II. On Sunday move to the Castello Sforzesco and the Pinacoteca di Brera. On Mondays the main museums are closed, and The Last Supper must be booked weeks ahead: it is not a last-minute Plan B.

Area
Central Milan: the Duomo area on Saturday, the Castello and Brera on Sunday
Duration
One weekend (two days), almost entirely indoors
Indicative costs
About 52 € in tickets for the Duomo (stairs combo), the Castello and Brera, plus 15 € for the Last Supper if booked (indicative averages)
Getting around
On foot in the centre, metro lines M1 and M3 for longer hops, contactless payment on ATM transport
To check
Monday museum closures, the compulsory Last Supper booking, Duomo opening hours

Rain does not cancel Milan; it just reshuffles it. The centre is packed with museums, the metro reaches almost everywhere, and the big sights sit in two fairly compact areas. All you need is a sensible order and a few checks on hours and closing days, because that is where a wet weekend really goes wrong.

How much does it actually rain in Milan?#

There is no "safe" month. According to the Italian Wikipedia entry on Milan's climate, average annual rainfall is about 920 mm over 82 days, with the peak in autumn, a secondary maximum in spring and a relative low in winter (around 40 mm in February). The same page notes that foggy days are becoming less frequent. These are climate averages, not a forecast: the practical point is that an October or November weekend in Milan can be dry or soaked, so have an indoor plan ready.

Rule one: one area per day#

The worst rainy-day route is one that crosses half the city at every stop. Here the advantage is clear: Saturday revolves around Piazza Duomo, where the Lombardia.info page puts Palazzo Reale about 120 metres from the Duomo and the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II about 250. On Sunday you shift towards the Castello Sforzesco and Brera. We did not verify the walking distance between the Castello and Brera, so check the map before you leave.

Try it

1. Duomo di Milano: Cathedral and MuseumIndoors#

The single ticket for the Cathedral and the Duomo Museum, including the church of San Gottardo in Corte, costs 10 € full price and 5 € reduced, according to the official access page in force since 1 April 2025. On Wednesdays the Museum is closed and the ticket drops to 8 € and 4 €. On a weekend that does not affect you. The Duomo's hours are not straightforward: the ticket office in the Sala delle Colonne is listed as open 9:00 to 18:00, with the last ticket at 17:50, while another line on the same page mentions access through the north portal from 8:00 to 19:00 with last entry at 18:10. Lombardia.info gives 9:00-19:00. The sources do not match, so to be safe arrive before 17:50. There is a second discrepancy about the Duomo Museum: the official site says it is closed on Wednesday, while Lombardia.info says it is open Tuesday to Sunday and closed on Monday.

2. The Duomo terracesOutdoors#

The terraces are the stop that suffers most in the rain. The official page mentions 135 spires, over 3,400 statues, a first level at about 31 metres and a central terrace at about 45, and says the mountains can be seen "on clear days". Official tickets for the terraces alone cost 18 € by lift and 16 € on foot (9 € and 8 € reduced). The Combo, which also includes the Cathedral and Museum, is 26 € by lift and 22 € on foot. We found no bad-weather closure rule on the pages we read: ask when you buy, or leave the climb for a clear day.

3. Galleria Vittorio Emanuele IIIndoors#

According to the Lombardia Beni Culturali catalogue entry it is a "covered pedestrian street" with an iron-and-glass structure on a cross-shaped plan, designed by Giuseppe Mengoni. Work began in 1865 and the gallery opened two years later, still unfinished; the complex was completed about twelve years after that. At the centre of the octagon there is a mosaic of the House of Savoy coat of arms. It is the right place for the ten minutes between visits, when the rain eases for a moment and you want to stay dry. We do not name cafés or shops here: we did not verify any.

4. Museo del NovecentoIndoors#

At Piazza Duomo 8, about 250 metres on foot from the Duomo stop on lines M1 and M3, according to the museum's site. Official hours: closed Monday, Tuesday to Sunday 10:00-19:30, Thursday until 22:30, last entry one hour before closing. We did not find ticket prices on the pages we opened, so we do not quote them. It is ideal for early Saturday because it does not force you to rush.

5. Castello Sforzesco: museums yes, courtyards noIndoors#

The Castello museums are open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00-17:30, with the ticket office closing at 16:30; the courtyards are free and open daily from 7:00 to 19:30, but they are outdoors. The ticket is valid for the whole day and covers all the museums. On the price the official page disagrees with itself: the body text says 10 € full and 5 € reduced, while the page description says 5 € and 3 €. Trust the body text, which is more detailed, and confirm at the ticket office. Every weekend at 11:00 there is a guided tour without booking, in English on Saturday and in Italian on Sunday, at 20 € with the museum ticket included. Entry is free for everyone on the first Sunday of the month.

6. Pinacoteca di BreraIndoors#

Official hours: Tuesday to Sunday 8:30-19:15, last entry at 18:00, closed on Mondays. The "La Grande Brera" ticket costs 20 € full and 4 € for ages 18-25; it covers the picture gallery and Palazzo Citterio, which you can visit the same day and in the following six days. On the first Sunday of the month entry is free with compulsory booking. The 8:30 opening makes it a good way to start Sunday before the crowds.

7. The Last Supper: only if you have bookedIndoors#

The Cenacolo Vinciano museum is open Tuesday to Sunday, 8:15 to 19:00 (last entry 18:45). The official site is blunt: booking is always compulsory, for everyone, even for free tickets. Sales open in three-month blocks, and the full ticket costs 15 €, 2 € for ages 18 to 25, free up to 18. The visit slot lasts 15 minutes and you must be at the ticket office at least 30 minutes before, or you lose your entry. If you do not already have a ticket, do not count on it as a Plan B.

How to order the weekend#

The real constraint is not the rain but the museum calendar. With these official hours, this order holds up best:

  1. Saturday morning
    Museo del Novecento

    Opens at 10:00 and closes at 19:30, last entry one hour earlier. If you have a free evening, Thursday it stays open until 22:30.

  2. Saturday midday
    Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II

    A few minutes' walk away: a covered pause with no rush.

  3. Saturday afternoon
    Cathedral and Duomo Museum

    Go in before 17:50. If the sky clears, consider the terraces.

  4. Sunday morning
    Pinacoteca di Brera

    From 8:30, last entry at 18:00: mornings are the calmest.

  5. Sunday afternoon
    Castello Sforzesco

    Museums close at 17:30 and the ticket office at 16:30: arrive by 16:00.

  6. Optional
    The Last Supper

    Only with a booking already in hand, arriving 30 minutes before your slot.

Tip

Buy the Last Supper and Brera tickets online weeks ahead, and keep the Duomo for a moment when you already have a free hour: if the weather turns, your real Plan B is always the Museo del Novecento or the Castello.

Getting around in the rain: ATM#

According to ATM, the single 90-minute ticket costs 2.20 €, the daily ticket (24 hours from first validation) 7.60 € and the 3-day ticket 15.50 €. Two daily tickets come to 15.20 €, almost the price of a 3-day ticket, so for a weekend choose by how you move: the ATM page adds that you can pay with a contactless card and that from the fourth journey the system applies the daily fare. The Duomo stop is on lines M1 (red) and M3 (yellow). On which tram lines stop at the Duomo the sources disagree: Lombardia.info lists 2, 12, 14, 16 and 19, while the Museo del Novecento lists 2, 3, 12, 14, 15, 16 and 19. For the right tram, use the ATM app.

How much it costs, on average#

Adding up the official prices read on the pages, a weekend with the Duomo stairs Combo (22 €), the Castello (10 €) and Brera (20 €) costs about 52 € per person, or 67 € with the Last Supper. These are indicative averages, excluding the Museo del Novecento, whose price we did not verify, and excluding transport and meals. The Castello page also lists the YesMilano City Pass Standard at 60 € for 3 days, which includes transport, the Duomo with stairs, the Castello, the Museo del Novecento and other museums: do the sums for your own list, because it only pays off if you visit most of it.

Common mistakes#

  • Arriving on a Monday with a museum-only plan: the Castello, the Novecento, Brera and the Last Supper are all closed.
  • Assuming you can walk into the Last Supper without a booking.
  • Counting on the terraces under a grey sky.
  • Reaching the Castello at 17:00: the ticket office closed at 16:30.
  • Trusting a single site for the Duomo hours: as we saw, the sources do not agree.

What to skip#

With only one rainy day, skip the Castello and keep the Duomo, the Galleria and one museum between the Novecento and Brera. If you have not booked the Last Supper, do not waste time hunting for it: build the weekend without it.

Frequently asked questions

What can you do in Milan in a weekend if it rains?

Spend Saturday around Piazza Duomo (Museo del Novecento, the Cathedral and Duomo Museum, Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II) and Sunday at the Castello Sforzesco and the Pinacoteca di Brera. Almost all of it is indoors, and the official hours list all of these as open on weekends.

Which Milan museums are closed on Monday?

According to the official sites we read, the Castello Sforzesco museums, the Museo del Novecento, the Pinacoteca di Brera and the Last Supper museum are all closed on Mondays. That is why a Saturday and Sunday is the easiest slot for a museum-heavy trip.

Do you need to book to see The Last Supper?

Yes, always. The official website says advance booking is compulsory for everyone and for every ticket type, free ones included, and that sales open in three-month blocks. You cannot improvise it on the morning.

How much is public transport in Milan?

According to ATM, a single 90-minute ticket costs 2.20 €, the daily ticket (24 hours from first validation) 7.60 € and the 3-day ticket 15.50 €. You can also pay with a contactless card, and from the fourth journey the system applies the daily fare. These are fares read on the ATM site and may change.

Is it worth going up the Duomo terraces in the rain?

Better not, if you have the choice: the official page says the mountains are visible on clear days, and the walkways are outdoors. If the sky is grey, keep the Cathedral and Museum and leave the terraces for another day.
