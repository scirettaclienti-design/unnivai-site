One day in Trieste: cafés, Opicina tram and bora plan B

Trieste9 minby Ivano ScirettaChecked on November 24, 2026

Quick answer

One day in Trieste rests on three blocks: a morning around Piazza Unità and one of the historic cafés listed by the City, then an afternoon on the Karst with the Opicina tram or at Miramare Castle (open 9 am to 7 pm, 12 € full price, park free but closing at 4 pm in November). On bora days the cafés and the castle are your fallback; the tram and the park can stop, as they did on 13 November.

Trieste makes sense through three things: a historic café, a place to look down on the city from, and the wind. The wind is the one that changes your plans. In a single week in November the park at Miramare closed for a weather alert and the Opicina tram stopped because a tree had fallen on its wires, so an itinerary written as if the sky never moves will not hold. This plan gives you one day with a choice of afternoon, and a plan B built around the bora.

  1. Morning
    Piazza Unità d'Italia and the pier

    On foot from the centre towards the sea, if the wind allows.

  2. Morning
    A historic café

    Specchi, San Marco, Urbanis, Tommaseo or Pirona: one or two are enough.

  3. Afternoon
    Opicina and the Karst

    Opicina tram, a walk on the panoramic road, back by tram.

  4. Afternoon (alternative)
    Miramare Castle

    Bus 6 or 36 or the train; arrive before 4 pm if you want the park.

Tip

Decide the afternoon in the morning, by looking at two things: the sky and Trieste Trasporti's notices about the tram. If the wind is strong, choose Miramare only for the castle (open until 7 pm) and keep the Karst and the pier for another day.

Where to start: Piazza Unità and the pier#

Piazza Unità d'Italia is the open-air living room facing the sea and the natural place to begin. It is also the square where Caffè degli Specchi stands (at number 7, according to the City) and where the City also places Harry's Grill, at number 2/1. The Molo Audace is a few steps away: in December 2024 InTrieste described residents and visitors who, after a night of bora, went there to look at the snow-capped mountains through the clean air of the gulf, with families in the square and photographers leaning into the wind.

Our rule is simple: if the gusts are strong, look at the pier from the square. We found no official rule forbidding it, so this is common sense, not a ban.

Which historic cafés to pick (and what the sources say)#

The City of Trieste publishes a historic-cafés itinerary with ten addresses. Five of them give you the most in one morning, all with a history you can check on the official page. Caffè degli Specchi, in Piazza Unità, opened in 1839 on the ground floor of a building above the remains of the medieval Amarina castle. Caffè Urbanis, at Piazza della Borsa 15, started in 1832 as a pastry shop and has a floor mosaic with mythological, marine and bora symbols. Caffè San Marco, at via Battisti 18, has furnishings designed in 1914 by Marco Lovrinovich and is described by the City as a haunt of artists and intellectuals. Pasticceria Pirona, at Largo Barriera Vecchia 12, opened in April 1900 and keeps its original furnishings with wooden and glass shelves; a portrait recalls James Joyce, a regular customer. Caffè Tommaseo, at Piazza Tommaseo 4/C, dates to 1825.

Here the sources disagree. The City dates Tommaseo to 1825, while ITALY Magazine says 1830; for Torinese the City says 1915 and the magazine 1919. We do not pick a year: if you need a date, use the City, which is the official source, and remember that its page was last updated in November 2020.

The practical advice is not to tick off cafés like a collection. One of the big ones (Specchi or San Marco) to sit down, and if time remains a smaller pastry shop such as Pirona or La Bomboniera in via Trenta Ottobre, which the City describes as having the original oven, one of the few of its kind still working in Italy. We did not read opening hours or prices on any official page, so we do not write them: check the hours for the day on a map app.

The Opicina tram: going up to the Karst#

The tram-funicular linking central Trieste with Villa Opicina, on the Karst plateau, has run since 1902 according to SiViaggia. The route is about 5 kilometres long and climbs 329 metres, all uphill. When it is running, it is a very Triestine experience, because the landscape behind you opens up at every bend.

The line has had a turbulent year, which is why the checked part is long. After months out of service, according to SiViaggia it restarted on 27 July 2026 with initial limits (no more than 80 per cent of capacity, trips from Piazza Dalmazia instead of Piazza Oberdan until the redevelopment works, expected between the end of 2026 and June 2027). Trieste Trasporti then announced a new restart on Monday 3 August, and on 9 August InTrieste reported another suspension for a fault in the cable section. The latest stop is from November: a fault found on the evening of Friday 13 November, caused, according to the technicians quoted by ANSA, by a tree falling on the overhead line between via Marziale and Conconello in the strong bora gusts. Service was restored on the morning of Tuesday 17 with the usual timetable. We did not read the ticket price or the first and last departures on an official page, and we do not know whether the departure point is still Piazza Dalmazia: check before you board.

At the top is the Opicina Obelisk, the terrace from which Trieste and the gulf are seen whole. From here starts the stretch of the CAI 1 trail described by Turismo FVG: the Vicentina road, known as the Napoleonica, which leads towards Prosecco with views over the city and the sea and, a little higher, the first of the three lookouts on the route (the Italia lookout). The full route to Aurisina is 17 km, takes about 4 hours 30 minutes and ends with bus 44 from the town hall square in Aurisina: for a single afternoon you do not need to do all of it. Walk as far as your time allows, then come back on the same tram. The source gives no length for the Obelisk-Prosecco stretch alone, and we do not invent one.

If you want another, more poetic face of the Karst, look at the Rilke Path near Duino-Aurisina: the FAI describes it as a wild, windy natural terrace above the sea, where Rainer Maria Rilke stayed between 1912 and 1922 and wrote the Duino Elegies. We did not verify how to reach it by public transport, so we leave it out of this plan.

Miramare: castle, park and how to get there#

Miramare Castle stands on a cliff over the gulf, about 8 km north-west of Trieste, built in white Istrian stone in an eclectic style. The official site lists the castle as open every day from 9 am to 7 pm (except Christmas Day and New Year's Day), with the ticket office closing at 6:30 pm. The park is free all year, but its hours change: in November and December it opens at 8 am and closes at 4 pm (in January it closes at 4 pm, in February at 5 pm). It is an easy detail to miss: the castle closes at 7 pm, but the park earlier, and the site describes about half a kilometre of paths between the park entrance and the castle, so it is better to arrive before 4 pm.

The ticket for the historical museum is 12 € full price, 2 € for young people aged 18 to 25, and on the first Sunday of the month entry is free: the next one is Sunday 6 December 2026. The audio guide costs 4 € for individuals, lasts about 60 minutes and comes in six languages. The museum warns on its site that parking is limited and invites visitors to use the bus, train or boat.

To get there without a car, the site lists three routes. By regional train (Udine-Trieste, Trieste-Venice or Trieste-Vienna lines) get off at Miramare station and walk about 150 metres along via Beirut to the park entrance. By bus, from the Central Station take lines 6 or 36 towards Miramare-Grignano: getting off at Bivio di Miramare leaves 750 metres; with line 6 to the Grignano terminus it is about 200 metres to the park and another 350 to the castle. The Delfino Verde boat, which runs from Trieste, Barcola or Sistiana to the bay of Grignano, is a summer service and not an option in November.

The bora: what really changes#

According to Meteored the bora is a katabatic wind, cold air flowing down from the Karst towards the Gulf of Trieste, typical of autumn, winter and early spring. Gusts often exceed 100 km/h and the records it cites reach 168 km/h (OSMER, 11 February 2012) and 188 km/h (10 March 2010, Istituto Nautico). This is not a figure to scare tourists: it is the reason Miramare park closed for the whole day on 13 November 2026 after weather alert no. 31/2026, with only the Parterre Café left open.

The bora also has a good side: it cleans the air and makes the gulf and the mountains sharp. For a visitor it means knowing in advance that the ideal day for the Karst and the pier is one without strong wind, and that on bora days you work indoors.

How much to spend (indicative averages)#

The figures we read: Miramare Castle 12 € full price (2 € from 18 to 25, free under 18 and on the first Sunday of the month), audio guide 4 €, park free. For an adult, a day with Miramare costs about 12 € in entry, plus the audio guide if you want it. We did not read the prices of the tram, city buses, the regional train to Miramare or a historic café, and we do not estimate them.

Common mistakes in Trieste#

  • Reaching Miramare after 4 pm and counting on the park: in November and December it closes before the castle.
  • Planning the tram without checking notices: it has stopped several times this year, and on 13 November because of the bora.
  • Trusting a single source for café dates: the City and ITALY Magazine do not agree on Tommaseo and Torinese.
  • Putting the Karst, the pier and Miramare into the same bora day: you lose the day and the wind decides for you.
  • Driving to Miramare: the site warns that parking is limited.

Plan B: when the day changes#

With the bora the plan still stands if you redraw the afternoon. Keep the morning in the centre between Piazza Unità and the cafés (look at the pier from the square), then go to Miramare Castle, which works indoors even in gusts, but check the notice on the site first: on 13 November the park was closed. If the tram is stopped, the Karst moves to the next day. If it rains without wind, the historic cafés and the castle work well indoors.

What to leave out#

  • Too many stops: the Karst and Miramare in the same afternoon are two half-days.
  • The car: it is not needed for Miramare or for the Karst, given the limited parking and the tram.
  • Places we do not know: apart from the historic cafés listed by the City we do not name restaurants, because we have not verified them.

Frequently asked questions

Can you see Trieste in one day?

Yes, if you pick one afternoon outside the centre. The morning is enough for Piazza Unità d'Italia, the waterfront pier and one or two historic cafés; the afternoon is either the Opicina tram with a walk on the Karst, or Miramare Castle, about 8 km north-west of the city. Doing both in the same day means rushing.

Is the Opicina tram running right now?

According to Trieste Trasporti, as reported by ANSA, service resumed on Tuesday 17 November 2026 after a fault caused by a tree falling on the overhead line the previous Friday. The tram has stopped several times this year (including in August), so check Trieste Trasporti's notices on the day.

How do you get to Miramare Castle without a car?

According to the official site, take a regional train to Miramare station and walk about 150 metres to the park entrance, then about half a kilometre to the castle. Alternatively take city bus 6 or 36 from the Central Station: with line 6 to the Grignano terminus it is about 200 metres to the park and another 350 to the castle. Parking at the castle is limited.

How much is Miramare Castle and when is it free?

The full ticket is 12 €, reduced to 2 € for EU citizens aged 18 to 25, and free under 18. Entry to the park is free all year. On the first Sunday of the month the castle is free for everyone: the next one is 6 December 2026.

What is the bora and does it change your visit?

It is a cold wind that blows down from the Karst towards the gulf, typical of autumn, winter and early spring, with gusts that often exceed 100 km/h according to Meteored. It changes the plan: on 13 November 2026 Miramare park closed because of a weather alert and the tram stopped after a tree fell. On bora days keep the cafés and the castle, and leave the Karst and the pier for another day.
