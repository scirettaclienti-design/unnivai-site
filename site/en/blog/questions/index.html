The most frequent questions about travelling in Italy, itineraries, rain and planning with AI, with short answers and links to the guides.

What do people eat for breakfast in Naples?

Coffee and a sfogliatella, either riccia (crisp, shell-shaped) or frolla (smooth and rounded). A Naples Insider guide says a sfogliatella generally costs no more than a couple of euros; that is an indicative price and varies by place.

What is a pizza a portafoglio?

It is a takeaway margherita a little smaller than the one served on a plate, folded in four and eaten by hand. According to Naples Insider it costs around €3, an indicative average.

Is STG Neapolitan pizza the same as any pizzeria's pizza?

No. STG is a European label, registered by Regulation (EU) No 97/2010, that sets rules for the dough, shape, ingredients and baking; a pizzeria that follows its own rules can still make excellent pizza, just without that label. The European specification and the AVPN association's rules do not match on everything, and we say where in the text.

Do you need to book at the historic pizzerias?

A Lost in Food guide says that in the historic pizzerias booking is often not possible and queues can be long, up to two hours in August. It is one visitor's experience, but it pays to arrive early or outside peak hours.

How much does it cost to eat in Naples?

Street tastings cost a few euros each (portafoglio, frittatina, crocchè, fried pizza). A simple takeaway pizza runs about €4-6 according to Naples Insider; a sit-down bill depends on the place. These are indicative figures, not promises.

How long do you need to visit the Sassi of Matera?

None of the official sources we read gives a duration. As an editorial estimate, one full day is enough to walk Sasso Barisano, the Civita and Sasso Caveoso with one or two indoor visits; you need two days if you add the Crypt of Original Sin and the museums. The Comune recommends doing the route on foot.

When is the best time to visit Matera?

Lonely Planet Italia names spring and autumn as the best seasons, with highs of 15 to 20 °C and little rain, and winter as the quietest period. A climate page we read gives July as the hottest month (average highs around 31 °C) and December as the wettest (75 mm over 9 days). These are climate averages, not forecasts.

How do you reach Matera without a car?

The Comune's tourist portal lists Bari Palese as the nearest airport, 55 km away, with a shuttle to Matera. By train the FAL line links Bari Centrale with Matera Centrale; the FAL station is not inside Bari Centrale and its entrance is at Corso Italia 8. Ferrandina-Scalo Matera is the nearest station on the national network, and from there you need a road connection.

Which Palermo market is best for street food?

It depends on the hour. According to Palermo Food Tours the Capo is one of the easiest to eat through, Ballarò is the oldest and largest and the most local, and the Vucciria is at its best in the evening. The Comune di Palermo describes them all as historic markets, together with Borgo Vecchio.

In Palermo it is arancina, feminine, according to Do Eat Better and Towns of Italy. In Catania, according to our guide, it is arancino.

What is pani ca meusa and is it worth trying?

It is a sandwich of spleen (and, according to one guide, lung), cooked in lard and served with lemon or caciocavallo. You order it schiettu (lemon only) or maritatu (with cheese). A Do Eat Better guide lists it at about €3 at a Vucciria stall; that is an indicative price.

Yes: the panelle and crocchè sandwich is, according to Do Eat Better, naturally vegan. Many arancine are vegetarian too, but the ragù ones contain meat and the «al burro» ones often contain cooked ham; always ask.

What are cicchetti and bacari?

Bacari are Venice's small traditional taverns; cicchetti are the small bites displayed on the counter, eaten with an ombra, a small glass of wine. People usually go from bacaro to bacaro, at lunchtime or aperitivo time.
