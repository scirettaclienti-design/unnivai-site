Questions people really ask us.

The most frequent questions about travelling in Italy, itineraries, rain and planning with AI, with short answers and links to the guides.

What is Catania's typical dish?

Pasta alla Norma: pasta with tomato sauce, fried aubergine, salted ricotta and basil. The name recalls the opera by Bellini, who was from Catania.

In Catania, is it arancino or arancina?

In Catania it is arancino, masculine, and traditionally cone-shaped, like Etna. In other parts of Sicily it is called arancina.

How much does it cost to eat in Catania?

Street tastings cost a few euros: an arancino around €2, granita with brioche around €4, a pasta alla Norma about €8 in simple places. Figures are indicative averages reported by Travel365 and vary from place to place.

When is it best to eat around the fish market?

In the morning, when the market is open: the street-food stands around it come alive around mid-morning. Our sources disagree slightly on opening hours (about 7-14 or 8-14:30), so arrive by mid-morning.

Are there vegetarian or gluten-free options in Catania?

Yes. Pasta alla Norma contains no meat, and Travel365 lists fully gluten-free places and places with plant-based menus. Always check ingredients and cross-contamination directly with the venue before you order.

Can I always find Bronte pistachio?

According to the protection consortium, Bronte green pistachio PDO is harvested only in odd-numbered years, and 2026 is an even year. Ask what is on the label and whether it is PDO.

Do I have to pay to enter Venice today?

No. On the City of Venice's official site the 2026 access fee trial has ended and from 27 July 2026 it does not apply. It returns in 2027 on fixed days from 26 March to 15 August, 8:30 to 16:30; amounts and procedures have not yet been published.

How much does the vaporetto cost in Venice?

On the official AVM site the ordinary 75-minute ticket costs 9.50 €; time-based tickets cost 25 € (24 hours), 35 € (48 hours), 45 € (72 hours) and 65 € (7 days). These are indicative prices: check the official page before you go.

When is Venice least crowded?

Guides point to early morning, evening after sunset and, outside holiday periods, the months from November to February. The city itself asks visitors to choose lower-traffic days and to stay more than one day.

What are cicchetti and bacari?

Bacari are Venice's small traditional taverns; cicchetti are the small bites displayed on the counter, eaten with an ombra, a small glass of wine. People usually go from bacaro to bacaro, at lunchtime or aperitivo time.

Is the Rialto market open every day?

No, and sources disagree about Saturday: one site gives the fish market as Tuesday to Saturday, another as Tuesday to Friday. Fruit and vegetables are listed as Monday to Saturday until 13:30. Go in the morning and check the day.

Can you see Lecce in one day?

Yes, if you choose. In a day you can fit the Roman amphitheatre, one museum, the single ticket for the baroque route (Duomo, Santa Croce and other churches) and the guided tour of the castle without rushing. The historic centre is walkable.

What is the worst day to go?

Monday. The Ministry of Culture page lists the amphitheatre as closed on Mondays, and the Charles V Castle is open Tuesday to Sunday. The Faggiano museum and the churches on the baroque route, by contrast, are listed as open every day.

How much is the ticket for the baroque churches?

On the official baroque route website the single adult ticket is 11 € (reduced 5 €, family 24 €); the full ticket with the climb up the Duomo bell tower is 21 €. These are prices read on the site today and can change.

What is a pasticciotto and when do you eat it?

It is a shortcrust pastry filled with custard and baked, typical of the Salento area and listed as a traditional agri-food product of Puglia. In the Salento it is usually eaten at breakfast, in cafés and pastry shops.

For the big classics yes, if you group them: Duomo and Accademia on one day, the Uffizi on the other. You will not see everything, and that is fine.

How much does the Uffizi cost?

On the official site the full ticket is 25 € if bought on the same day and 29 € if bought in advance. From 4 pm there is an Afternoon promotion: 16 € (20 € if bought in advance). These are prices read today and can change.

How do you visit Florence Cathedral?

The Cathedral itself is free to enter. For the other monuments in Piazza del Duomo you need one of the official Passes; the Brunelleschi Pass covers the Dome, Bell Tower, Baptistery, Museum and Santa Reparata and is valid for 3 calendar days.

Do you need to book the Dome?

Yes: the Dome climb has a time fixed at purchase, with a maximum 5-minute tolerance, and since 1 March 2025 an ID document is required. There are 463 steps and no lift.

The sources we consulted indicate opening Monday to Saturday and closing on Sunday, with slightly different hours: three say about 7 am to 2 pm, one says 8 am to 2:30 pm. The practical rule is to go by mid-morning: after noon many stalls start to pack up.

Can I visit when it rains?

Does it rain a lot in Catania?

Rain falls mostly between autumn and winter and often arrives in downpours. One climate page we read lists about 7 rainy days in October and 8 in each of December and January, against 1 in June and July: treat these as orders of magnitude, not a forecast. It is worth having an indoor plan B for half a day instead of cancelling everything.

Yes, but wear the right shoes: the lava paving gets slippery. Alternate short outdoor stretches with indoor visits that are close to each other, instead of crossing half the city.

One day is enough for the main stops on foot. The island is a little over one square kilometre and the stops are close together. With half a day you have to choose between the Duomo, the market and the seafront.

Yes. The island is small and linked to the mainland by two bridges, Ponte Umbertino and Ponte Santa Lucia. A restricted traffic zone covers the whole island, so comfortable shoes matter more than any vehicle.
