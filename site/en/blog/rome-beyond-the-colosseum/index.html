What to see in Rome beyond the Colosseum: 5 areas

Rome9 minby Ivano ScirettaChecked on November 10, 2026

Quick answer

Five areas south of the centre: Testaccio (market, Mattatoio, Non-Catholic Cemetery), Ostiense (Centrale Montemartini, the Gasometer, street art), Garbatella, San Paolo and the Appian Way Regional Park. On Mondays Centrale Montemartini and the Mattatoio are closed: that day pick the cemetery, Garbatella and the park.

The Colosseum is a short walk from the centre, but Rome does not end there. To the south, between the Tiber and the Appian Way, there is a different city: a neighbourhood that grew around a slaughterhouse, a power station turned museum, a village of low houses with courtyards. These areas work better as two half-days than as a rush, and they have a practical advantage: fewer queues and simpler opening hours.

  1. Area 1 · Testaccio
    Market, Mattatoio and Non-Catholic Cemetery

    Rome's working-class neighbourhood: market in the morning, then two places with different hours.

  2. Area 2 · Ostiense
    Centrale Montemartini and the Gasometer

    Ancient statues in a power station: the city's most unusual museum.

  3. Area 3 · Garbatella
    Lotti, courtyards and Piazza Sant'Eurosia

    Low houses with gardens, built in the 1920s.

  4. Area 4 · San Paolo
    Basilica and Parco Schuster

    One of the four papal basilicas, outside the walls.

  5. Area 5 · Appian Way
    The Appian Way and the Caffarella Valley

    Europe's largest urban protected area, on foot or by bike.

Tip

Put the stop with the strictest hours at the end of your plan, or earlier in the day: the Non-Catholic Cemetery closes at 5 pm (last entry 4:30 pm), Centrale Montemartini at 7 pm. If you start at the market in the morning, you have time for both on the same day.

How do you get around this part of Rome?#

The easiest starting point is metro line B, Piramide stop. According to Turismo Roma, the stop is about one kilometre from Testaccio, and the centre is about half an hour away on foot, passing the Circus Maximus to Piazza Venezia. That is why Testaccio makes a good first half-day: start there, then move towards Ostiense and Garbatella, which are close to each other.

We did not read an official timetable for services linking all the areas, so we do not give frequencies or journey times we cannot verify. For the Appian Way, the park says it can be reached by bus and metro and that, once there, you move on foot or by bicycle; information centres and bike-rental points are listed on the park's website.

Testaccio: why it is Rome's most Roman neighbourhood#

Testaccio is the district where you can tell that people really live in Rome. Turismo Roma describes it as a neighbourhood with working-class roots, shops with marble signs and a square, Piazza Testaccio, with the Fountain of the Amphorae. The name comes from broken pots: Monte Testaccio, also called Monte dei Cocci, is a hill formed by discarded amphorae piled up since Roman times. The same page says it can be visited by reservation, but we found no up-to-date information from its operator: check before you plan around it.

Mattatoio. The former slaughterhouse, in use until 1975, is now a cultural space run by Azienda Speciale Palaexpo, with temporary exhibitions and installations. Turismo Roma's listing gives opening from 11 am to 8 pm, last entry 7:30 pm, closed on Mondays. What you will see depends on the current programme: check which shows are on.

Nuovo Mercato Testaccio. It stands opposite the Mattatoio and, according to Turismo Roma, it is best visited in the morning, among stalls of fruit, bread, meat and fish and food kiosks. The same source mentions a tripe or boiled-beef sandwich as a typical bite, and notes that ancient walls are visible underfoot in a few spots. We give no opening hours because we did not read them on an official market page.

Non-Catholic Cemetery. From the market you follow via Zabaglia and then via Caio Cestio, a short walk from the Pyramid of Cestius. The official site gives regular hours of Monday to Saturday 9 am to 5 pm (last entry 4:30 pm) and Sundays and public holidays 9 am to 1 pm (last entry 12:30 pm). It adds a warning: in case of a weather alert the cemetery may close at short notice. It has been in use since 1716 and holds more than 6,000 burials of over fifty religious denominations and nearly sixty countries. Turismo Roma mentions the tombs of Keats, Shelley, Gramsci and Camilleri. We did not read the entry price, so we do not state one.

Where to eat. Turismo Roma describes Testaccio as one of the evening nightlife hubs, with osterias competing over cacio e pepe, carbonara, pajata and amatriciana. We name no restaurants because we verified none: choose by looking at the posted menu and at who is sitting at the tables.

Ostiense: the industrial Rome you did not expect#

Ostiense took shape in the early 1900s, when mayor Ernesto Nathan started a new industrial area here. The General Markets (opened in 1921), the River Port, the Gas Works with the Gasometer of 1936 and Centrale Montemartini, the city's first public electricity plant, followed.

Centrale Montemartini. It is the second exhibition site of the Capitoline Museums and, according to Turismo Roma, it holds a large share of the ancient sculptures found in excavations between the late 1800s and the early 1900s. In the Machine Room the statues stand beside turbines, diesel engines and the huge steam boiler. Pieces mentioned include the pediment statues from the Temple of Apollo Sosianus, the colossal acrolith of the goddess Fortuna from Largo Argentina, the muse Polyhymnia and a large hunting-scene mosaic from Santa Bibiana.

The address is via Ostiense 106. On the official site the hours are Tuesday to Sunday, 9:00-19:00, with last entry one hour before closing; closed on Mondays, 1 May and 25 December; 24 and 31 December 9:00-14:00. For tickets, the official page lists free entry for residents of Rome and its Metropolitan City with an ID card. For non-residents it lists 11 EUR full price and 6.50 EUR reduced; further down there is another pair of prices (15.50 EUR and 11.50 EUR) without a clear explanation, so we do not choose for you: check the page or ask at the desk. There is also a combined ticket with the exhibition "Moda in luce 1955-1975", until 15 November 2026: 14.50 EUR full and 10 EUR reduced for non-residents. These are indicative prices, to be rechecked.

Gasometer and street art. The Gasometer is an iron structure just under 90 metres high and more than 60 metres wide, nicknamed the "industrial Colosseum" for its monumentality. Turismo Roma also points to the street art on buildings and warehouses: "Hunting Pollution" by Iena Cruz on via del Porto Fluviale, "Black&White Power" by Sten&Lex on via dei Magazzini Generali and, opposite, the "Wall of Fame" by JB Rock.

Garbatella: a village with courtyards#

Garbatella was born in the 1920s. Turismo Roma describes it as a neighbourhood built on the model of English garden cities, with houses in lotti (blocks) for workers of the via Ostiense industries, and notes that it was first called Concordia. Italia.it explains that the buildings are no more than three storeys high and surrounded by gardens and inner courtyards, with washhouses and drying areas as in a village. The best-known block is lotto 24, with thirteen villas in Roman "barocchetto" style, the "model houses", marked at the entrance with the architect's name.

What to look at: Piazza Sant'Eurosia, whose arch Nanni Moretti rode through on a Vespa in Caro Diario; the Fontana Carlotta with the "lovers' staircase"; the "scoletta" in Piazza Nicola Lombardi; Villa Rosselli, built on a Roman domus of the 1st century AD. One note: the two sources spell the name of the square of the Fontana Carlotta differently (Rinaldo da Montecroce on Italia.it, Ricoldo da Montecroce on Turismo Roma), so search the map for "Fontana Carlotta" rather than the square's name. Turismo Roma also mentions the Alberghi Suburbani, colourful buildings that housed families displaced after the war, and the former Palladium cinema, now a theatre.

San Paolo: the basilica outside the walls#

Between via Ostiense and the Tiber stands the Basilica of St Paul Outside the Walls, one of Rome's four papal basilicas and the second largest after St Peter's. It was rebuilt between 1825 and 1854 after the fire of 1823; the interior has five aisles divided by eighty monolithic granite columns. Outside, the cloister dates from the 13th century and in front of the basilica is Parco Schuster, with the "Steel Forest" (nineteen steel menhirs, 4 metres high, in memory of the Nassiriya victims) and the archaeological area of the Ostiense Necropolis, from the 2nd-3rd century AD. We did not read the basilica's opening hours on an open page during our research: check the official site before you go.

The Appian Way: the park that reaches almost into the centre#

The Appian Way Regional Park describes itself as the largest urban protected area in Europe: 4,580 hectares running from the centre towards the Alban Hills. It includes the Appian Way, with more than 16 kilometres you can walk, the Caffarella Valley (200 hectares) and the Aqueducts area (240 hectares). The official site runs a programme of guided visits every spring and autumn. We did not read the hours of individual archaeological sites (catacombs, mausoleums): for those use the "Aree e siti monumentali" section of the park's website.

The most common mistake is treating the park as a half-hour stop. Pick one sector and stay there. Also check the park's news: on 20 October 2026 it published a weather alert for Lazio, and outdoor routes change with the rain.

How do you combine the areas?#

A simple split, using only verified hours: first half-day, Testaccio (market in the morning, Mattatoio from 11, the Non-Catholic Cemetery before 4:30 pm) and then Ostiense (Centrale Montemartini, last entry 6 pm). Second half-day: Garbatella on foot and then San Paolo, or the Appian Way if you have energy. If you only have one day, keep Testaccio and Ostiense: they are the two areas with the most recognisable character.

Monday changes everything: Centrale Montemartini and Mattatoio are closed. In that case do the Non-Catholic Cemetery, Garbatella and the Appian Way park. On Sundays remember the cemetery closes at 1 pm.

What if it rains?#

The indoor spaces on this itinerary are Centrale Montemartini, the Mattatoio and the Basilica of St Paul: move your rainy day there. The Non-Catholic Cemetery and the Appian Way park are outdoors, and the cemetery closes during weather alerts. Garbatella is walked among its courtyards, but it is nicest in good weather.

What to skip#

Three choices that lighten the day. First: do not try all five areas together. Second: do not go to Ostiense on a Monday for the museum. Third: do not plan a lunch without checking the menu and prices, especially in the evening in Testaccio, when it becomes a nightlife hub.

Frequently asked questions

What is there to see in Rome besides the Colosseum and the historic centre?

South of the centre: Testaccio (market, Mattatoio, Non-Catholic Cemetery), Ostiense (Centrale Montemartini, the Gasometer, street art), Garbatella (a 1920s neighbourhood built in "lotti"), the Basilica of St Paul Outside the Walls and the Appian Way Regional Park. They are best explored on foot, with far fewer queues than the centre.

How much does Centrale Montemartini cost?

The official site lists free entry for residents of Rome and its Metropolitan City with an ID card, and for non-residents 11 EUR full price and 6.50 EUR reduced. The same page also shows a second pair of prices (15.50 EUR and 11.50 EUR) without clearly saying which case it applies to, so ask at the ticket desk or check the page before you go.

Which days are the museums in this area closed?

According to the official pages, Centrale Montemartini is closed on Mondays (and on 1 May and 25 December) and the Mattatoio is closed on Mondays. The Non-Catholic Cemetery is open Monday to Saturday, which makes it the right stop for a Monday in the south.

Can you visit Monte Testaccio?

The Turismo Roma page says it can be visited by reservation, but we did not find up-to-date hours or booking details from its operator. If you want it on your plan, check before you travel.

Is Garbatella a good place for a walk?

Official sources describe it as a human-scale neighbourhood, good for a stroll among its lotti, courtyards and gardens. We found no safety data and do not write any: as in any residential area, go in daylight and take the usual precautions.

How long do you need for the Appian Way Regional Park?

It depends on what you choose: the park covers 4,580 hectares and the Appian Way has more than 16 kilometres you can walk. Pick one sector in advance (the Appian Way or the Caffarella Valley) and check the park's website for the hours of individual sites.
