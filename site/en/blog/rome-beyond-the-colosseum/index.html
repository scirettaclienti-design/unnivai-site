What to see in Rome besides the Colosseum: 5 areas

Rome9 minby Ivano ScirettaChecked on November 10, 2026

Quick answer

Five areas outside the classic circuit: Trastevere and Tiber Island on the right bank of the Tiber, Testaccio, Ostiense and the Aventine on the left. Opening hours set the order: Centrale Montemartini is closed on Mondays, Testaccio Market on Sundays, and the Orange Garden closes at 18:00 (6 pm) until February.

If you search for what to see in Rome besides the Colosseum, you usually get a list of monuments. Here we think in areas, which is how you really walk: five pieces of the city, each with one clear reason to go and an opening time that can wreck your plan if you ignore it. These are not hidden neighbourhoods, but they are places where you spend less time in queues and more time looking around.

  1. Morning
    Aventine

    Orange Garden and the view over the Tiber, open until 18:00 in winter.

  2. Late morning
    Testaccio Market

    Open Monday to Saturday, 7:00-15:30.

  3. Early afternoon
    Non-Catholic Cemetery

    Last entry at 16:30 (Sundays at 12:30).

  4. Afternoon
    Centrale Montemartini

    Tuesday to Sunday, last entry one hour before the 19:00 closing.

  5. Evening
    Trastevere and Tiber Island

    The right time for the square and the riverbank.

Tip

On Mondays Montemartini is closed and on Sundays the market is not there: plan Testaccio and the Aventine for the days when the other pieces fall out, and keep Montemartini for a Tuesday-to-Saturday.

Trastevere: what is there to see beyond the nightlife?#

Turismo Roma describes it as one of the best-loved neighbourhoods, on the right bank of the Tiber, with lanes, squares and trattorias serving traditional Roman cooking, and it is also known for its nightlife. But it is above all an area of churches and art: the same page lists Palazzo Corsini with the National Gallery of Ancient Art and the Botanical Garden in its grounds, Villa Farnesina with frescoes by Raphael and his school, and the basilicas of Santa Cecilia and San Crisogono.

The heart is the basilica of Santa Maria in Trastevere. According to the official page it was probably the first official place of Christian worship in Rome; tradition credits Pope Callixtus I in the 3rd century and says it was finished in 340, but that is a legend and should be taken as one. What you see is more concrete: the apse mosaics, with the stories of the Virgin by Pietro Cavallini (1291) and those from around 1140 in which Pope Innocent II holds a model of the church; twenty-two ancient granite columns, probably from the Baths of Caracalla; the gilded ceiling designed by Domenichino in 1617.

Watch the hours: the official page does not give them and refers to the basilica's contacts for Masses and how to visit. So we cannot tell you how late you can go in. The advice is to phone (06 5814802, the number on the page) or to go in the morning, and keep the square for the evening.

Tiber Island: how big is it and what is there?#

We did not read walking times, but the measurements explain why it is more a pause than a stop: Tiber Island, the only urban island in the river, is about 300 metres long and about 90 wide, linked to Trastevere by Ponte Cestio and to the old Jewish Ghetto by Ponte Fabricio.

The legend told by Turismo Roma ties it to a plague in 291 BC: the delegation sent to Epidaurus, a cult site of Aesculapius, the god of medicine, came back by ship with a snake, which jumped into the water near the island; where it took shelter a temple was built, today replaced by the church of San Bartolomeo all'Isola. Like every legend it is undocumented, but it explains the ship-like shape well. The best way to do it is to pair it with Trastevere: you cross one bridge on the way there and the other on the way back.

Testaccio: is it only for eating?#

Testaccio is on the left bank of the Tiber and for Turismo Roma it is the "true popular soul" of Rome. Its symbol is the amphora: Monte dei Cocci (Mons Testaceus) is an artificial hill made from the shards of millions of amphorae once used to bring food and drink into the city. At the foot of the hill, caves that used to be cellars and stables now hold restaurants and bars. We name none: we have not verified them.

For simple eating there is Testaccio Market, open Monday to Saturday from 7:00 to 15:30, with entrances from via Beniamino Franklin, via Alessandro Volta, via Aldo Manuzio and via Lorenzo Ghiberti. It is closed on Sundays. In practice it is a stop for the morning or lunchtime: after 15:30 there is nothing left to look for.

Testaccio also has the Mattatoio (the former slaughterhouse), described as an industrial-archaeology building now given over to contemporary art, and the archaeological area of the Emporium, the ancient river port of the 1st century AD. We did not read hours for these two: check first.

Non-Catholic Cemetery and Pyramid of Cestius: what can you visit?#

The cemetery yes, the pyramid normally no. The Non-Catholic Cemetery (via Caio Cestio 6) holds the graves of artists and poets, including John Keats and Percy Bysshe Shelley. According to the official site it is open Monday to Saturday from 9:00 to 17:00, with last entry at 16:30; on Sundays and public holidays from 9:00 to 13:00, last entry at 12:30. The site also lists closures: 1 and 6 January, fifteen days around mid-August, and 8, 24, 25, 26 and 31 December; at Easter, Easter Monday, 25 April, 1 May, 2 and 29 June it closes at 13:00. It may close for reasons of force majeure without notice.

It is still an active burial ground: the site asks for respect, notes that the ground is uneven in places and recommends comfortable shoes. The entrance fee does not appear on the pages we read, so we do not write it.

The Pyramid of Cestius stands right next to it. According to Turismo Roma it is 36.40 metres tall, with a base of about 30 metres a side, and it is the tomb of Gaius Cestius, built between 18 and 12 BC; an inscription says the work had to take no more than 330 days, on pain of the heirs losing the inheritance. Four centuries later it was built into the Aurelian Walls. You can see it from the street and from the cemetery, but the site can only be visited on special openings and during events: do not count on going in. To get there, the cemetery site lists Metro line B, Piramide stop, along with several bus lines.

Ostiense: is Centrale Montemartini worth the trip?#

It is the stop least like what you expect from Rome. Centrale Montemartini (via Ostiense 106) is a branch of the Capitoline Museums: the photo shows ancient statues next to the big industrial machines of the hall. The contrast is the reason to go.

Hours on the museum site: Tuesday to Sunday, 9:00-19:00, last entry one hour before closing. Closed on Mondays, 1 May and 25 December; on 24 and 31 December 9:00-14:00. The full ticket is 11 EUR and the reduced one 6.50 EUR, with a 1 EUR pre-sale fee if you buy online or through the call centre (indicative averages). Since February 2026 entry has been free for residents of Rome and the Metropolitan City, showing an ID card. The site's New Year entry is still the 2026 one: for 1 January 2027 we have no data.

Aventine: the Orange Garden and the famous keyhole#

Parco Savello, or the Orange Garden, stands on the Aventine on the site of a fortress built by the Savelli family towards the end of the 13th century, itself on a 10th-century castle of the Crescenzi. From the viewpoint over the river the view runs from the bend of the Tiber to St Peter's Basilica. The orange trees were planted in memory of St Dominic, who founded the convent here in the 13th century; the current park dates from 1932, designed by Raffaele De Vico.

Turismo Roma's hours are useful but unclear: daily, October to February 7:00-18:00; March to September 7:00-20:00; April to August 7:00-21:00. The last two bands overlap, so for summer the page points to the City of Rome page it cites. For now, in November, what counts is that the park closes at 18:00: if you want sunset from the viewpoint, you will not get it here.

Leaving the garden and turning right you reach Piazza dei Cavalieri di Malta, designed by Giovan Battista Piranesi in 1765. Through the keyhole of the gate of the Villa del Priorato you can see the dome of St Peter's framed by the hedges. We did not read whether you can go into the villa: the game is to peek through the hole.

How do you combine the five areas without rushing?#

The order proposed above is built around opening hours, not distances: we did not read travel times between one area and the next, so check the map and decide whether to split it into two half days. With two days, a simple idea is one for the left-bank areas (Aventine, Testaccio, Ostiense) and one for the right bank (Trastevere and Tiber Island).

The common mistake is to leave Montemartini for last, on a Monday, or the Orange Garden for after 18:00: those are two times people forget. Another is to turn up at Testaccio Market late in the afternoon.

Frequently asked questions

What is there to see in Rome besides the Colosseum?

Five areas that mass tourism barely touches: Trastevere with the basilica of Santa Maria, Tiber Island, Testaccio with its market and the Non-Catholic Cemetery, Ostiense with Centrale Montemartini and the Aventine with the Orange Garden. Each one holds half a day.

Is Centrale Montemartini open on Mondays?

No. The museum site lists Tuesday to Sunday, 9:00-19:00 (9 am to 7 pm), with last entry one hour before closing. It is closed on Mondays, 1 May and 25 December; on 24 and 31 December it opens 9:00-14:00.

Is Testaccio Market open on Sundays?

No. The market site lists Monday to Saturday, 7:00-15:30 (7 am to 3:30 pm), with entrances from via Beniamino Franklin, via Alessandro Volta, via Aldo Manuzio and via Lorenzo Ghiberti.

Can you go inside the Pyramid of Cestius?

Normally no. The Turismo Roma page, updated in September 2026, says the site can only be visited on special openings and during events. You can see it well from the Non-Catholic Cemetery, but you cannot reach the pyramid from the garden.

What are the opening hours of the Orange Garden?

The Turismo Roma page lists daily opening, 7:00-18:00 from October to February. For March-September the same page gives 7:00-20:00 and then 7:00-21:00 from April to August, with overlapping dates: check the city page before you go.

What are the opening hours of the Non-Catholic Cemetery?

Monday to Saturday 9:00-17:00 with last entry at 16:30; Sundays and public holidays 9:00-13:00 with last entry at 12:30. The site also lists closures, including 1 and 6 January and 8, 24, 25, 26 and 31 December.
