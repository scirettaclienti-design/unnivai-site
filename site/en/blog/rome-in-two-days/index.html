Rome in two days: an itinerary that does not wear you out

Rome7 minby Ivano ScirettaChecked on October 28, 2026

Quick answer

Day one: ancient Rome. Day two: historic centre and Vatican, in an order that depends on bookings. Always check opening hours and access rules.

You can see Rome in two days if you accept one rule: do not try to see everything. The city is made of layers, and the more you try to cover it, the more time you spend between stops. The secret is to group it by area.

  1. Day 1 · morning
    Colosseum, Forum and Palatine

    A single route: book ahead to avoid the queue.

  2. Day 1 · lunch
    Monti

    A more genuine neighbourhood a few minutes from the Colosseum.

  3. Day 1 · afternoon
    Capitoline Hill and the centre

    The view over the Forum, then on foot into the historic centre.

  4. Day 2 · morning
    Vatican Museums and St Peter's

    To be booked, and the longest part of the day.

  5. Day 2 · afternoon
    Pantheon, Navona, Trevi

    All close, a few minutes' walk apart.

  6. Day 2 · evening
    Trastevere

    A stroll with no plan.

Tip

Put the booked stops on the same day and keep the other one free: if it rains or you are tired, you move the free part, not the one with a ticket.

Which day do you need?

Day 1: ancient Rome#

Morning: Colosseum, Forum and Palatine. They are close and are often visited on a single route: check the official site for how tickets and hours work. Booking ahead avoids the worst of the queue.

Lunch around Monti. A few minutes' walk from the Colosseum, it is a neighbourhood with less touristy places than the area immediately around the monuments.

Afternoon: Capitoline Hill and the centre. Climb the Capitoline Hill for the view over the Forum and continue on foot towards the historic centre.

Day 2: historic centre and Vatican#

One decision matters here: the Vatican takes time and usually a booking. If you want to see it, put it in the morning and keep the rest of the day lighter.

Morning: Vatican Museums and St Peter's. Check closing days and entry rules on the official site.

Afternoon: Pantheon, Piazza Navona and Trevi. They are all close, a few minutes' walk from one another. For the Pantheon, check the entry rules.

Evening: Trastevere. A stroll with no plan is the right way to finish.

The three rules that make the difference#

  1. Group by area. No zig-zagging.
  2. Book the most requested places, leave the rest free.
  3. Leave one empty hour each day. Rome gets better when you are not in a hurry.

Frequently asked questions

Are two days enough for Rome?

Two days are enough for the great classics if you group them by area. They will not be enough for everything, and that is fine.

Should you book in advance?

For the most requested places, like the Colosseum and the Vatican Museums, usually yes: check availability and entry rules on the official websites.

What is the most common mistake?

Jumping from one side of the city to the other. Grouping stops by area saves hours of walking.
