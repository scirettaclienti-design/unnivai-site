Method and sources

How we write

A travel guide is only as good as the data behind it. These are the rules we apply to every article on the blog.

Open sources, cited

Every practical detail (opening hours, prices, access rules, connections) is read on pages we actually opened: websites of city councils, museums, parks, tourist boards, transport operators, UNESCO and, when nothing better exists, industry publications. Sources are listed at the bottom of every article. If a source cannot be reached we use another; if we cannot confirm a detail, we do not write it.

When sources disagree

It happens often, especially with opening hours. When it does we say so in the text, give both versions, and point to the official page to check before you go. We do not quietly pick the version that seems more likely.

Prices and hours

Prices are always indicative and hours change with the seasons: every article shows the date of its last check. We do not name restaurants or shops unless we read them on an open source, and in that case we say which one.

Photos

Photographs are real images under free licences (Wikimedia Commons), with author and licence in the caption. We do not publish AI-generated images presented as photos of places.

The role of artificial intelligence

UNNIVAI builds products with artificial intelligence and we say so: research and first drafts of the guides are assisted by AI tools, and every article passes an automated check (length, sources from several domains, FAQ, internal links) before publication. AI can be wrong, which is why the data rests on verifiable sources and not on a model's memory alone.

Found a mistake?

Write to sciretta.clienti@gmail.com with the page and the detail: we check it, correct it and update the article's verification date.

What about the app?

The blog guides are independent of the UNNIVAI app. In the future some may link to routes prepared in the app: we will always flag that explicitly.
